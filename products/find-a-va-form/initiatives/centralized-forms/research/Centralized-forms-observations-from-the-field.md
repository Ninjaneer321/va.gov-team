## Top of mind observations

**Observation #1: Most participants took a search-first approach, many aided by AI.**

- Participants often used benefit + task phrasing first, only using form numbers if provided. They use Google + AI or VA.gov search depending on habit and confidence. 
- When users can’t quickly confirm they’re in the right place, they return to search (often Google again) rather than reading more on the page.
> P6: “If I didn’t find it on the VA app… I’d just do a Google search for it and see what came up.”
> P1: “I think we’d started just with Google… Google Chapter 35… it took me to the VA website…”

**Observation #2: Participants looked for a fast, obvious way to start the form (CTA-first; minimal reading). All participants successfully identified how to start, but strong preference for online completion/submission - PDF/mail-ONLY creates friction and can cause abandonment.**

- Participants often scan quickly and go straight to the action (usually “apply/file online”), treating the rest as optional.
- PDF-only or mail-only steps are viewed as outdated and burdensome; printer access and extra steps are major barriers.
- Having submission options *in addition* to online was a nice to have, and seen as the VA trying to make benefits more accessible to all. 
> P1: “I typically skip the part where it talks about evidence, and just jump right into the form.”
> P4: “I don’t even care about anything else… what I really need is just to complete it.”
> P5: “Probably stop right there… Nope.” (re: only mail option)
> P12: "You can tell that the VA wants you to participate in their system here...they put a lot of thought into giving people lots of different options so that they can choose which one works for them. It's not… ‘this is our system, if you don't like it, get out.’ It's, ‘hey. We want you to use our program, so there's a hundred ways to do it.’”

**Observation #3: Participants confirm form applicability through quick matching: the form's plain language title indicating the benefit + the task/scenario they’re trying to do, only confirming by number when given it by a notification. They struggled to do this quick matching when the information was in paragraph text.**
-  When they couldn't quick match, they either (a) start the form anyway to find out, (b) go back to search/Google, or (c) lose trust.
- This happened in different ways for ALL the forms we tested. When they cover multiple scenarios (initial application + benefit change for Education form 1990 overlaps with defunct 1995; initial application + increase for 526EZ) and also when it was part of a multi-form workflow or highly related (Mandatory verification of dependents vs. add/remove). People consistently missed the content that let them know whether the form applied in the when to submit section. 
> P4: “It doesn’t seem like it is a form for an increase… I think it’s just for initial application.” (missed bullet point in When to Use indicating it was for an increase)
> P6 (dependents wording): “The title is a little misleading… ‘Verification of Dependents for Disability Benefits.’ My dependents don’t have disability benefits…”
> P3: “They told me to use this form, and… then okay, I would go… punch in that number to bring it up.”

**Observation #4: Participants expected “What to know before you start” to be a concrete checklist (docs/info needed), not vague guidance**
- Participants want specific, actionable “have these ready” bullets; vague promises (“we’ll tell you…”) reduce confidence.
> P10: “It says… we’ll tell you what information or documents you need… but I don’t really see that listed here.”
> P8: “It’d be nice to have that there… ‘You’ll need your bank account routing number… your new school… their mailing address…’”

**Observation #5: The “download/open PDF help” microcopy is often ignored as fluff by confident users, but becomes relevant only when something breaks (and can confuse less tech-savvy users)**
- More confident users skim past PDF help text and only use it if the download fails. Some were confused between 'free pdf reader' and 'browser', not recognizing the difference. Some thought that the note was specific to this form only and wouldn't be fillable on the computer rather than general helper text. And some completely ignored it and were confident they could convert the pdf to fillable even if the VA seemed to be indicating they could not.
> P6: “The whole use a free PDF reader… that’s gonna confuse older people.”
> P11: "That note is for other people."

**Other Observations/Limitations**
> Did not see much usage of breadcrumbs or navigation between pages since users were not actually filling forms. Instead, lots of scrolling for CTA's if navigating the benefit hubs. 
> Discussion of authenticated experience and app expectations around forms.
> Travel Pay forms were consistently mentioned, including one deep dive discussion
> 526 was almost always singled out as 'special'/using a VSO even among confident, tech-savvy users
> Many users stopped after identifying the online CTA + Download CTA and did not notice the accordion or other ways to submit headers
> Certain types of "after you submit' information was considered helpful in the event of deadlines (particularly around education), but most did not continue to think through 'after submitting' when in the mindset of getting started.
