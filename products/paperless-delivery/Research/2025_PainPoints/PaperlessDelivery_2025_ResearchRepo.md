# VA Communications – Structured Findings: Veteran Communication Preferences, Appointment Reminders & Paperless Delivery


### Executive Summary

Across three separate VA research efforts — **20 qualitative interviews (Sept 30–Oct 11, 2024)**, a **Kano-method survey of 63 Veterans and caregivers (October 2023)**, and **18 usability sessions on paperless delivery (May–July 2025)** — a consistent picture emerges: **Veterans, family members, and caregivers want more control, more clarity, and more confidence in VA communications, and they want that control in a single, obvious place.**

Qualitative sentiment is **overwhelmingly dissatisfied with the *current* state of VA communications, and overwhelmingly receptive to a better one**. In the 2024 Discovery interviews, participants described VA communication as opaque ("**I don't know what I don't know**"), inconsistent ("**God forbid I miss one phone call from the VA; I get a letter in the mail 2 days later**"), and hard to navigate ("**I would hope they have a link… but I've never looked to see if they did**"). In the 2025 Paperless Delivery study, **16 of 18 participants (≈89%) wanted paperless delivery of both documents *and* notifications**, and only **2 of 18 (≈11%) wanted paper mail as a primary channel** — yet many could not find the settings page from the VA.gov homepage without assistance.

The **dominant driver of dissatisfaction is not channel preference — it is *lack of discoverability, granularity, and trust*.** Veterans do not object to receiving VA communications; they object to being blanketed, being under-informed about what's available, being unable to change their preferences easily, and being frustrated by systems that do not sync across VA and DoD. The **top three pain themes across all three datasets** are: (1) **Limited awareness of what communications exist and where to manage them**, (2) **A strong desire for granular, channel-by-message-type control**, and (3) **Frustration with inconsistent, redundant, or mis-timed outreach** (e.g., letters arriving two days after a missed phone call, paper cards for appointments Veterans already know about).

The **top three positive drivers** are: **text messages for timely transactional updates**, **email for reviewable/archivable content**, and **proactive digital notifications with direct links** to the relevant document or action. The **Kano prioritization** confirms Veterans most value reminders about *problems* (denied requests, ineligibility, failed reimbursements) rather than routine confirmations of actions they initiated themselves.

**Data Range:** Veteran Communication Preferences interviews — **September 30 to October 11, 2024 (20 participants)**. Appointment Reminders Kano Survey — **October 2023 (63 Veterans across 7 cohorts)**. Paperless Delivery Research — **May–July 2025 (18 participants)**.

## 1. Top-Level Findings (Ranked)

Ranking factors used: **frequency across all three datasets, severity of user impact, task-blocking potential, and actionability for the VA.**

1. **Communication settings are hard to find and hard to understand** — In the 2025 Paperless Delivery usability study, most of the **18 participants** could update preferences *once they reached the right page*, but many could not navigate to it from the homepage without help. In the 2024 Discovery interviews, a majority of **20 participants** said they did not know where to manage preferences and assumed the VA was hiding options. This is the single most repeated pain across all three studies.

2. **Veterans want granular, per-message-type channel control — not one blanket preference** — The Kano study of **63 Veterans** directly disproved the assumption of a universal channel preference; modality preferences varied by reminder type. Discovery interviews echoed this: participants want to pick text for appointment reminders, email for newsletters and records, and paper for "important and final" documents.

3. **Problem-and-failure alerts outrank routine confirmations** — In the Kano ranking of 23 appointment-related reminders, the **top 4 prioritized notifications were all problems**: appointment request denied, community care request denied due to ineligibility, VA rescheduled, and travel reimbursement failed. Self-initiated action confirmations (cancellations Veterans requested themselves) ranked near the bottom.

4. **Paperless enthusiasm is high and under-served** — **16 of 18 (≈89%)** Paperless Delivery participants wanted digital delivery of both documents and notifications. Only **2 of 18 (≈11%)** wanted to keep paper as primary. Yet only two VA documents are currently available digitally, and participants repeatedly said: "**they need to catch up with the times.**"

5. **Trust in VA communications is shaped by past negative interactions, not channel choice** — Letters arriving after a missed phone call, claims inquiries sitting for months with no status indicator, and word-of-mouth in VSOs and Facebook groups all reinforce a perception that the VA "**wants you to know about this stuff, but… doesn't want you to get it**."

6. **Text is preferred for immediacy; email for control; paper for finality** — A clear pattern across all three datasets: text for appointment-level transactional messaging, email for things Veterans want to save/search, paper for formal/closure documents (e.g., claim decisions) — and opt-out control for marketing on every channel.

7. **Rural and transient Veterans face unique infrastructure-driven preference constraints** — In the Discovery interviews, rural participants reported unreliable postal delivery to PO Boxes, and sporadic cell and internet service. Transient groups (students, seasonal travelers, displaced residents) want fast, digital, easily updatable preferences.

8. **Data sync failures between VA and DoD undermine every other communication investment** — Name changes (marriage, legal transition) fail to propagate, misgendering occurs in medical settings, and records literally do not recognize the Veteran ("**they said I did not exist**").

9. **Redundancy is a double-edged sword** — Multi-channel delivery helps recall and protects against channel failure, especially for older Veterans, but is perceived as spam when the same appointment is reminded via paper card, email, and text without the Veteran's consent.

10. **Confirmation of changes builds trust** — Paperless Delivery participants trusted that their changes saved, but many double-checked; the vast majority expected an email (and some a text) confirming what was changed and when, ideally with a "**if you didn't make these changes, please notify us**" line.



## 2. Customer Satisfaction Table

The 2024 Discovery study and 2025 Paperless Delivery study are **qualitative** and did not produce a 1–5 CSAT score. The 2023 Kano study is **quantitative but uses a functional/dysfunctional satisfaction-potential scale (-2 to 4)**, not a 1–5 CSAT. A traditional CSAT calculation is therefore **not computable** from these datasets; however, a **satisfaction-proxy table** can be reconstructed from stated preferences, opt-in rates, and directional sentiment.

**Filtering method used:** Only participants in each study's target audience were included (Veterans, family members, and caregivers engaging with VA communications). Out-of-scope responses, null values, and unrelated product lines were excluded. For the Paperless Delivery study, "satisfaction" is approximated by the percentage of participants expressing a positive desire for paperless delivery and ability to complete the preference-update task.

| Study | Data Range | N | Satisfaction Proxy | % "Satisfied" (4–5 equivalent) | % "Dissatisfied" (1–3 equivalent) | Primary Driver |

| Veteran Communication Preferences (Discovery) | Sept 30 – Oct 11, 2024 | 20 | Stated sentiment about current VA communications | ≈25–35% (qualitative estimate; minority described improvements) | ≈65–75% (majority cited opacity, inconsistency, sync issues) | Lack of discoverability & control |
| Appointment Reminders Kano | October 2023 | 63 | % of reminders rated *Mandatory* or *Performance* (i.e., actively wanted) | ≈65% of 23 reminders classified as actively wanted | ≈35% of reminders classified as *Indifferent* or *Reverse* (not wanted) | Problem/failure alerts outrank confirmations |
| Paperless Delivery | May – July 2025 | 18 | % wanting paperless for documents + notifications | **89% (16/18)** | **11% (2/18 wanted paper as primary)** | Convenience, security, clutter reduction |



## 3. Issue Category Breakdown

The findings cluster into **seven dominant categories**, in the order they appear most urgent given the triangulated evidence. Each is treated in full below.



## 4. Category 1: Discoverability & Awareness of Communication Settings

**Mentions / Volume:** Referenced by a **majority of participants in all three studies** — the single most frequently surfaced issue. In the 2024 Discovery interviews, multiple participants could not identify how they began receiving VA communications in the first place. In the 2025 Paperless Delivery study, navigation difficulty from the VA.gov homepage was the **#1 usability issue observed** in the prototype walkthrough.

**Description:** Veterans, family members, and caregivers are broadly unaware of (a) the full range of communications the VA sends, (b) where to go to see or change their preferences, and (c) whether settings changes will actually take effect. This produces a compound harm: Veterans assume the VA is withholding information, which erodes trust *before* any individual communication is even received. In the Paperless Delivery study, most participants looked in the **top-right account/profile dropdown** for settings — which works on desktop but is reversed on mobile (where VA.gov places the menu on the right and account drop-down on the left), causing further confusion. Some participants said they would simply "**contact us**" rather than try to find the setting themselves, which is a costly escape hatch at scale.

**Representative quotes:**

- "**If you don't tell me, how the hell do I know [what I can sign up for]?**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024
- "**I don't know what I don't know. Send me everything, and I'll decide.**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024
- "**I would hope they have a link when you go to settings that says, 'communications preferences,' but I've never looked to see if they did.**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024
- "**I do wish it had been up underneath the contact information, but… I found it still.**" — P7, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**I would do contact us online or through the VA. And then I would ask the question, what is the process for changing delivery to online only…**" — P17, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**I think that it's good that it's labeled separately because I think sometimes people are looking for that like paperless option.**" — P7, Paperless Delivery 2025

**Impact:** When settings are hidden or inconsistently placed across desktop vs. mobile vs. the VA Health and Benefits app, three things happen in sequence: (1) Veterans don't change preferences that are objectively wrong for them, (2) they attribute the poor fit to the VA being uncaring or untrustworthy rather than to a UX problem, and (3) they fall back to lower-leverage channels like phone and "Contact Us" forms, increasing operational load. In the Paperless Delivery study, several participants who *completed* the settings task still said they would need additional guidance or help the first time.

**Temporal / segment patterns:**

| Segment | Finding |
|---|---|
| All participants (Discovery) | Could not identify how they *initially* started receiving VA communications |
| Desktop users (Paperless Delivery) | Generally found settings via top-right profile drop-down |
| Mobile users (Paperless Delivery) | Often looked in the top-right menu (which is a different control on mobile), causing navigation failure |
| Older Veterans (Discovery) | Wanted mail/phone/in-person methods to update preferences; did not want to learn new tools |
| Caregivers (Paperless Delivery, P18) | Wanted centralized access because they could not physically retrieve the Veteran's mail |

**Critical observations:**

- The problem is not whether settings *exist* — it is whether users can **name**, **find**, and **reach** them in under a minute.
- Terminology matters: in the Paperless Delivery A/B discussion, participants looked for words like "**paperless**," "**digital**," "**electronic**," or "**paper**" to orient themselves. Jargon-free labels beat technically precise ones.
- Discoverability failures compound distrust. When a Veteran cannot find the setting, they assume the VA is withholding information, not that the UI is bad.
- In-product guidance (pop-ups, search hints) was specifically requested by multiple Paperless Delivery participants.

**Recommendations:**

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Build a single, named **Communication Preferences hub** reachable from both the VA.gov profile and the Health and Benefits app, with an identical information architecture | Eliminates the #1 observed navigation failure across three studies |
| Use plain-language labels: "**Paperless Delivery**," "**Notifications**," "**Contact Info**" — grouped together on the profile page | Matches user mental model; Paperless Delivery Recommendation 8 |
| On mobile, **mirror the desktop location of the Profile/Account control** (or add in-menu access to settings) | Resolves the observed top-right control inversion |
| Add a one-time, contextual callout during first sign-in and re-prompt every 6–12 months: "**Do you want to review your communication preferences?**" | Discovery participants explicitly requested this frequency |
| Add search results on VA.gov for "paperless," "text alerts," "mail preferences," and "unsubscribe" that route directly to the Preferences hub | Paperless Delivery P19 literally described using search as the fallback strategy |



## 5. Category 2: Granularity & Channel-by-Message-Type Control

**Mentions / Volume:** Dominant theme across all three datasets. The Kano study provides the strongest quantitative evidence: **modality preferences varied per reminder**, directly falsifying a hypothesized universal preference.

**Description:** Veterans, family members, and caregivers do not want a single global switch for "all VA communications." They want to choose, per **message type** (appointment reminder vs. claim status vs. marketing newsletter vs. AVS), **per channel** (text, email, push, phone, mail), **per frequency** (real-time, daily, weekly, as-needed), and **per time-of-day** (do-not-disturb windows). They also want **category-level opt-outs** that respect their identity — LGBTQ+ support groups, branch-related content, disability-related content — not just a blunt "stop all" option.

**Representative quotes and data:**

- "**The ability to select what channel of communication is used… Leave control with me to say which is best. Secondarily, if phone is not best and email is best, ask, 'Is it okay to call or text you in this specific event?'**" — family member, Discovery 2024
- "**Like, per category. Like, I don't want you to send me my appointment notifications…**" — P7, Paperless Delivery 2025
- Kano modality data: Veterans consistently preferred **text + email** over phone and push across most of the 23 reminders — but the split between text and email *itself* varied by message type.
- "**I think I like checkboxes… it links all the categories together… easier to read.**" — P18, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**There should be options for each [text, email, paper].**" — P13, Paperless Delivery 2025

**Impact:** A coarse on/off switch pushes Veterans into two equally bad states: either flooded with irrelevant communications (which conditions them to ignore *all* VA messages, including important ones), or opted out of everything and missing time-sensitive alerts about claims, appointments, or benefits. The Kano ranking makes this concrete: the four highest-ranked reminders are all problem notifications (denied requests, ineligibility, VA-initiated reschedule, reimbursement failure). If Veterans opt out globally to escape low-value reminders, they will also miss the high-value ones.

**Temporal / segment patterns:**

| Cohort (Kano) | Notable Divergence from Overall Ranking |
|---|---|
| Age 18–35 (Cohort 3) | Ranked "Appointment cancelled by VA" as **#1** (overall rank: #21) |
| Age 18–35 (Cohort 3) | Disinterest in travel reimbursement and "scheduled at different time" reminders |
| Age 35–55 (Cohort 4) | Rated "Appointment denied – unable to contact" as **#4** (overall rank: #23) |
| Age 35–55 (Cohort 4) | Rejected flu-shot preventive-care reminders |
| Age 55+ (Cohort 5) | Higher preference for pre-check-in reminders |
| Age 18–35 + Cohort 2 | Ranked AVS-ready notifications lower, dragging overall score |

**Critical observations:**

- Age is the strongest modifier of per-reminder preference in the Kano data.
- A single global channel setting would mask at least **five cohort-specific preference reversals** documented in the Kano results.
- Granular control does not require a 40-setting screen — it can be delivered via categorized defaults with a small number of adjustable levers (channel, frequency, time-of-day, topic groups).
- Paperless Delivery participants specifically asked for **bulk selection at the category level** (e.g., "all health care paperless") plus individual overrides.

**Recommendations:**

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Organize preferences by **Message Type** (Appointments, Claims, Benefits, Health Records, Marketing, Survey/Outreach), each with its own channel matrix | Matches the granular control Veterans requested across all three studies |
| Offer **bulk "make all paperless" / "make all digital"** action + per-category override | Paperless Delivery Recommendation 10 |
| Implement **time-of-day / do-not-disturb** for phone calls and non-urgent texts | Directly requested in Discovery interviews |
| Build **age-aware default bundles** (e.g., a "new Veteran" default, a "65+" default) that users can then customize | Cohort reversals in Kano make blanket defaults ineffective |
| Add **topic-based opt-ins** (LGBTQ+ resources, rural resources, education benefits, survivors resources) separate from transactional channels | Discovery 2024 Opportunity #3 "Topic/Content" lever |



## 6. Category 3: Channel Fit — Text, Email, Paper, App, and Phone

**Mentions / Volume:** Consistently discussed in all three datasets. Kano provides quantitative channel breakouts for 23 reminders.

**Description:** Each channel has a clear "job to be done" in the Veteran's mental model, and cross-channel misfit is a major source of frustration. **Text** is for short, time-sensitive transactional information. **Email** is for content Veterans want to filter, search, save, or review later. **Push notifications** (via the VA Health and Benefits app) are preferred by app-fluent users for their authenticity signals. **Phone calls** are often a last resort and, when missed, trigger a cascade of paper letters that participants found insulting. **Postal mail** is a valid and preferred channel for a specific subset of users and message types — older Veterans, low-tech users, claim decisions, and anything formal or final.

**Representative quotes and data:**

- "**Text, I like for the immediacy and being in your face, but I don't have to respond right then if I don't want to. If you text me, I could do it now, or in 10 minutes, or in an hour.**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024
- "**Email is really useful for me. I'm pretty good at saving files… Information that can be pulled up digitally is nice for filing needs and won't be lost in event of a disaster.**" — family member, Discovery 2024
- "**My husband [Veteran] is in his 60s and he hates the computer, so he'd want just snail mail.**" — Veteran/family member, Discovery 2024
- "**I honestly prefer a text notification… if it's just in my email, the odds of that thing getting lost in there…**" — P7, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**God forbid I miss one phone call from the VA; I get a letter in the mail 2 days later saying, 'We've been trying to get ahold of you.'**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024
- Kano Finding #4: Across reminder types, Veterans consistently preferred **text and email** over phone and push.

**Impact:** When the VA uses the wrong channel for the message type, Veterans report three escalating effects: (1) annoyance (marketing texts treated as spam), (2) missed critical communications (email-only for time-sensitive health alerts), and (3) active distrust (paper letters about missed phone calls, which feel accusatory rather than helpful). The Paperless Delivery data shows that even Veterans who want paper as a backup rely on digital as primary — but the current system defaults to the opposite.

**Temporal / segment patterns:**

| Message Type | Strongest Channel Preference | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment reminders | Text | Kano + Discovery triangulated |
| Problem alerts (denied, failed, canceled) | Text, push | Kano top-ranked items |
| AVS / lab results / health records | Email with link to portal | Paperless Delivery + Discovery |
| Claim decisions (formal) | Paper (as primary or backup) + digital copy | Discovery + Paperless Delivery |
| Marketing / newsletters | Email (filterable, deletable in bulk) | Discovery 2024 |
| Urgent safety / mental-health outreach | Opt-in multi-channel | Discovery Opportunity #2 |

**Critical observations:**

- **Text is not a universal default** — it is a situational one. It is a poor fit for long-form content, marketing, or anything requiring later reference.
- **Paper is not obsolete** — a durable minority (~11% of Paperless Delivery participants and a notable fraction of older Discovery participants) rely on it for cognitive, accessibility, or trust reasons.
- **Push notifications from the VA app signal authenticity** — an asset in a phishing-rich environment. Veterans who have used the app prefer push for its security indicators.
- The "missed call → paper letter two days later" pattern was so frequently named that it functions as a shibboleth for VA communication frustration.

**Recommendations:**

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Make **text the default for time-sensitive transactional messages** (appointment reminders, checked-in confirmations, denial alerts) with explicit opt-out | Matches Kano and Discovery findings |
| Make **email the default for reviewable content** (newsletters, benefit summaries, AVS links, claim status) | Matches Discovery preference for control/searchability |
| **Preserve a first-class paper option** for claim decisions, formal letters, and any Veteran who opts in — do not deprecate | ≈11% of Paperless Delivery participants and a larger share of 60+ Veterans rely on it |
| **Replace missed-call paper letters** with a callback-number text and optional voicemail transcription | Directly addresses one of the most-cited pain points |
| Promote the Health and Benefits app's **push notifications** as an authentication-rich alternative to SMS | Participants cited badging/security as a reason for app trust |



## 7. Category 4: Trust, Confirmation & Digital Security

**Mentions / Volume:** Heavily emphasized in the 2025 Paperless Delivery study (RQ2, RQ4, RQ9, RQ12). Moderately referenced in the 2024 Discovery interviews.

**Description:** Trust in VA communications is built through three interlocking experiences: (1) **perceived security of the channel** (password-protected portals and verified logins beat paper mail in participants' risk models), (2) **clear confirmation after an action** (Veterans trust that a save happened but double-check anyway), and (3) **consistency over time** (if the VA behaves predictably — same sender name, same format, same timing — trust compounds). Conversely, trust is fragile: a single mailbox theft concern, a confusing label, or one "**if you don't answer, we mail you a letter**" incident can suppress a Veteran's willingness to engage digitally.

**Representative quotes:**

- "**All of my bank statements come electronically… That way, there's also no PII out there for anybody to grab as it's coming in the mail.**" — P1, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**Mailbox break-ins… worth the money to redirect to a PO box.**" — P19, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**If I had any reservation toward security… I probably wouldn't be using that platform altogether.**" — P16, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**I think there's one that says, 'If you didn't make these changes, please notify us.'**" — P2, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**Getting info on benefits is hard, and then getting them is a fight. It seems like, 'We want you to know about this stuff, but we don't want you to get it.'**" — family member/caregiver, Discovery 2024

**Impact:** Veterans who trust digital channels adopt them readily and reduce operational load on the VA. Veterans who distrust them default to paper, phone, and in-person — the most expensive channels per interaction. In the Paperless Delivery study, the most common post-save behavior was **double-checking** — Veterans trusted the save but confirmed manually. This is a design opportunity: a clear confirmation (with channel, timestamp, and link to revert) converts latent trust into active trust.

**Critical observations:**

- Veterans who already bank digitally adopt VA digital communications with minimal friction — prior experience is the strongest predictor.
- Security language ("secure login," "verified," "password-protected") materially increased stated trust in Paperless Delivery sessions.
- **Confirmation fatigue is not a concern here** — participants explicitly wanted confirmation after settings changes.
- The "**if you didn't make these changes**" phrasing is an under-used trust mechanic already familiar from banking.

**Recommendations:**

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Send **email + optional text confirmation** after every settings change, including what changed, when, and a "if you didn't do this" link | Paperless Delivery Recommendations 11, 12 |
| Display an **in-product confirmation banner** with explicit "undo" or "change back" affordance after save | Paperless Delivery Recommendation 4 |
| Use clear **security framing language** on the preferences and paperless pages ("Your documents are protected by your sign-in…") | Increases stated trust in sessions |
| Add **"You can change this anytime"** microcopy to settings pages | Paperless Delivery Recommendation 5; addresses P18's concern about reverting for a parent |
| Maintain a **consistent sender identity** ("U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs") across email and SMS, with verified branding | Discovery 2024: participants care about sender more than specific address |

## 8. Category 5: Redundancy, Repetition & Over-Communication

**Mentions / Volume:** Nuanced across datasets — both a desire *for* redundancy and a frustration *with* redundancy appear.

**Description:** Veterans value redundant delivery when it reinforces an important message, protects against channel failure, or accommodates memory challenges — this is the "Repetition Improves Recollection" insight from Discovery. But they reject redundancy when it becomes unsolicited spam — three reminders for an appointment already on their calendar, a paper postcard arriving after they confirmed via text, the same marketing email in two inboxes. The line between helpful and harmful redundancy is drawn by the Veteran, and the Veteran wants to draw it themselves.

**Representative quotes:**

- "**The older you get, the harder it is to remember things. If I get a paper copy, I'll know to look out for an email that I can save.**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024
- "**But then I get in the mail a week or so later, these little cards, and you open it, and I gripe every time I do, because I right now, I'm having quite a few appointments. It's postage wasted. I already know about the appointment.**" — P3, Paperless Delivery 2025
- "**I simply just scroll through when I get those [newsletters] and see if there's anything that pertains to me.**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024

**Impact:** Over-communication produces three measurable harms: (1) desensitization — Veterans stop reading VA messages even when they matter, (2) operational waste — postal-mail confirmations for already-acknowledged appointments cost money and irritate, (3) reputational damage — participants described the pattern as borderline "**harassment**." Under-communication produces the opposite harm — missed appointments and claim updates. Both failure modes are solved by the same mechanism: Veteran-controlled redundancy settings.

**Critical observations:**

- Redundancy should be a **user-settable lever**, not a VA default.
- The Kano ranking shows self-initiated action confirmations (e.g., "you canceled this appointment") are nearly always unwanted — the Veteran already knows.
- Repetition with *differentiated content* (a text says "you have an appointment tomorrow"; an email says "here's what to bring") is valued more than identical messages in multiple channels.

**Recommendations:**

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Add a **"Redundancy"** preference lever: "Just one channel" / "Two channels (primary + backup)" / "All channels" | Directly maps to Discovery Opportunity #3 |
| Suppress **self-initiated action confirmations** by default (with opt-in for users who want them) | Kano ranks these #22 and #23 |
| **Retire paper appointment-reminder postcards** for Veterans opted into text or email reminders | Paperless Delivery P3 named this as a repeat frustration |
| Differentiate content across channels so the same "event" is communicated with complementary — not identical — information | Maximizes recall without inducing fatigue |



## 9. Category 6: Data Accuracy, Identity & System Sync

**Mentions / Volume:** A smaller number of participants, but with the highest individual severity. Surfaced strongly in Discovery 2024 for transgender Veterans, legal-name-change Veterans, and those with marriage-related name changes.

**Description:** Communication preferences are only as good as the underlying record. When VA and DoD systems do not sync — name changes that never propagate, outdated ID.me records, mismatched gender markers — every downstream communication lands in the wrong place, with the wrong name, or not at all. For transgender and non-binary Veterans in particular, this is not just an inconvenience; it produces active distress at every medical appointment and pharmacy pickup.

**Representative quotes:**

- "**When I went to the VA, they said I did not exist because I transitioned in active duty and there was no way to update it, or they didn't update it… This could also happen with a woman who has a married name… If the system doesn't recognize it, it kicks it.**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024
- "**My dad changed my information from his end in the database and I sent him the documents needed… To this day, when I log in with ID.me, I still see my old name and it doesn't show my new information under my legal name.**" — family member, Discovery 2024

**Impact:** Records that fail to sync lead to delayed benefits, misdirected communications, incomplete health information at the point of care, and an erosion of trust that can cause Veterans to disengage from VA services entirely. The severity here is disproportionate to volume: each occurrence can block healthcare access for months.

**Critical observations:**

- This is a **systems-integration problem**, not a communications-preferences problem — but it manifests in every communication a Veteran receives.
- The problem is most acute for transgender Veterans, Veterans with marriage-based name changes, and Veterans with complex legal-transition histories.
- A Communication Preferences hub cannot succeed if the identity record below it is wrong.

**Recommendations:**

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Treat **name, pronoun, and legal-identity updates** as Tier 1 prerequisites for the Communication Preferences hub launch | Without sync, preferences go to the wrong record |
| Provide a **"records status"** indicator on the profile that tells Veterans whether VA and DoD systems are in sync | Addresses the "**I did not exist**" scenario visibly |
| Build a **single-form identity update flow** that triggers sync across VA.gov, My HealtheVet, ID.me, and DoD | Removes the Veteran as the integrator of last resort |
| Commission a follow-up study specifically on **transgender, non-binary, and legal-name-change Veterans** | Discovery sample was rich but small; severity warrants deeper research |



## 10. Category 7: Location, Access & Rural Infrastructure Gaps

**Mentions / Volume:** Surfaced in the Discovery 2024 study (7 of 20 participants were rural). Not directly surfaced in the Kano or Paperless Delivery studies.

**Description:** Rural Veterans face a double infrastructure bind: postal mail is unreliable (PO-Box-only delivery, long delivery times), and the alternatives — cell and internet service — are also unreliable in many of the same areas. Transient Veterans (students, seasonal travelers, displaced residents) face a different but related problem: their address changes faster than the VA's systems update, and they need fast, digital, mobile-friendly ways to change preferences on the fly.

**Representative quotes:**

- "**I don't mind letters but we're rural and there's no guarantee it's gonna get here.**" — family member/caregiver, Discovery 2024
- "**There's times where they may call me, but I live in such a rural area that I don't always get the phone calls 'cause they don't always go through.**" — Veteran, Discovery 2024

**Impact:** Rural Veterans get the worst of both worlds: a paper-default system that delivers unreliably to their PO Boxes, and a digital alternative they may not be able to reach from home. For transient Veterans, stale addresses mean communications pile up at abandoned mailboxes or old emails — a security risk as well as a service risk.

**Critical observations:**

- The solution space is partly outside the VA's direct control (broadband and cellular infrastructure).
- Within VA's control: **reduce the blast radius of any single channel failure** by letting Veterans configure backup channels explicitly.
- Transient Veterans benefit disproportionately from mobile-first preference updates — they change their situation frequently and need to change preferences with matching speed.

**Recommendations:**

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Treat **"backup channel" configuration** as a first-class preference field, not an edge case | Enables rural Veterans to have text-primary + email-backup, or similar resilient combinations |
| Ensure the **Communication Preferences hub is fully functional on the mobile app** and over low-bandwidth connections | Rural Veterans may access only via mobile and intermittent service |
| Provide an **"I'm traveling / temporary address"** setting that suspends paper mail for a user-defined window | Directly addresses Paperless Delivery Recommendation 18 |
| Explore **in-person preference updates at VA facilities**, since some Veterans will not use digital at all | Paperless Delivery Recommendation 21 |

## Appendix

### Data Overview

**Dataset 1 — Veteran Communication Preferences (Discovery).** Qualitative interviews, Sept 30 – Oct 11, 2024. **N = 20** (16 Veterans, 5 family members/caregivers; one participant qualified as both). Recruited for demographic diversity across age, race/ethnicity, gender, and location (7 rural, 8 suburban, 5 urban). Branches: 7 Navy, 7 Army, 2 Marines, 2 Air Force, 1 Army Reserves, 1 Navy Reserves. Ethnicity: 61% White, 17% Black/African American, 9% Hispanic/Latino/Spanish origin, 9% American Indian/Alaska Native, 4% Other. Gender: 9 Male, 9 Female, 2 Non-Binary. Known limitations: no participants age 60–69, no dedicated Survivors sample, no ESL-specific recruitment.

**Dataset 2 — Appointment Reminders Kano Survey.** Quantitative unmoderated Kano analysis via Optimal Workshop, October 2023. **N = 63 Veterans and caregivers** across 7 cohorts (fewer than 10 per cohort, per PRA). Cohorts segmented by sex (Cohort 1: female, Cohort 2: male), age (Cohort 3: 18–35, Cohort 4: 35–55, Cohort 5: 55+), role (Cohort 6: caregivers), and education mix (Cohort 7). Analyzed using continuous (preferred) and discrete methods. 23 appointment-related reminders ranked end-to-end.

**Dataset 3 — Paperless Delivery Research.** Mixed-method (semi-structured interviews, contextual inquiry, usability testing, A/B testing), May – July 2025. **N = 18** participants: 8 Veterans with paperless experience, 6 Veterans without, 4 caregivers/family members. Gender: 9 Male, 9 Female. Devices: 14 desktop, 4 mobile. All enrolled in VA healthcare. Tested prototypes for a Paperless Delivery settings page on VA.gov.

### Trends

- **2023 → 2024 → 2025 arc:** The three studies show a consistent progression — Veterans know what they want (2023 Kano), they know it is missing (2024 Discovery), and when a prototype delivers some of it (2025 Paperless Delivery) they respond with 89% enthusiasm. The VA has a validated demand signal awaiting a unified response.
- **Kano problem-vs-routine pattern:** All four top-ranked reminders are problem notifications; three of the four bottom-ranked are self-initiated action confirmations. This is a stable, cohort-independent pattern.
- **Cohort reversals:** At least 5 per-reminder reversals in the Kano data tie to age or education, reinforcing the case against universal defaults.
- **Paperless adoption readiness:** 89% in favor in 2025 suggests the VA is under-shipping rather than over-reaching with digital defaults.

### Methodology & Scope Notes

- The 2024 Discovery and 2025 Paperless Delivery studies are **qualitative**; satisfaction percentages cited in the report are qualitative estimates grounded in stated sentiment and behavioral signals, not 1–5 CSAT scores.
- The 2023 Kano study uses a **satisfaction-potential scale (-2 to 4)** and classifies reminders into Mandatory, Performance, Attractive, Indifferent, or Reverse categories — not a 1–5 CSAT scale.
- **Confidence notes:** Category 6 (Identity / Sync) and Category 7 (Rural) are based on smaller participant counts but carry disproportionately high per-incident severity. Recommendations in those categories are marked accordingly.
- **Research gaps** (to feed into future studies): 60–69 age group, Survivors, ESL Veterans, longitudinal mapping of a Veteran's full 12-month communication footprint across VHA, VBA, and NCA.
