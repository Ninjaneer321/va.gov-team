# **VA Home Loan Guaranty Program – Structured Findings: COE, Loss‑Mitigation & Servicing Support (Call‑Center)**


## Executive Summary
Across the **COE and mortgage servicing support** calls, veterans report **high friction** in loss‑mitigation (VASP/forbearance transitions), short‑sale/deed‑in‑lieu processing, **COE issuance/eligibility**, and **appraisal/property condition** disputes—often compounded by servicer delays and **poor courtesy/communication**. Satisfaction (CSAT) was **not collected** in this dataset; however, completion success was **mixed**: many contacts end with referrals or escalations rather than resolution. Foreclosure‑related issues dominate early 2025, while **COE & eligibility** emerges as the top monthly driver by late spring. The **VASP program shutdown (May 1, 2025)** is a pivotal event frequently cited by callers as creating a **gap with few viable alternatives**, elevating risk of foreclosure and homelessness. **Data range**: January 2, 2025 to June 2, 2025 (analysis window) with records present through February 27, 2026 in the file.

## 4. Category 1: Foreclosure & Loss Mitigation / VASP
**Mentions / Volume:** ~26.3% of analyzed calls.

**Description:**  
Veterans frequently report **confusing or stalled transitions** out of COVID‑era forbearance, **denials or missed windows** for the **VASP** program, and unaffordable modifications at **7%+ interest** that double monthly payments. Many cite **unclear waterfall steps**, repeated documentation requests, or servicer refusal to return required VASP packets to VA. 

**Representative quotes or metrics:**
- “**VASP** paperwork was returned to **US Bank**, but they never sent it back to VA; no timeline exists—‘US Bank can take as long as they like.’”   
- “**Wells Fargo** wants about **$60,000** or a refinance at a much higher rate; if not, consider selling or deed‑in‑lieu.”   
- “VA said they would **purchase my home**; a month later they **backtracked**, and now records **show missing purchase decisions** in VALERI.” 

**Impact:**  
High likelihood of **foreclosure progression**, **credit damage**, and **housing instability**—especially where servicer timelines or program endpoints (e.g., **VASP shutdown May 1, 2025**) intersect with real‑world crisis events (job loss, medical issues). 

**Temporal or segment patterns:**
- **Jan–Apr 2025:** Foreclosure/Loss‑mitigation dominates monthly drivers.  
- **Post‑May 1, 2025:** Callers increasingly reference **program closure** and lack of alternatives. 

**Critical Observations:**
- **Program gaps** post‑VASP create a **hard cliff**—callers often told only to sell, short‑sell, or accept unaffordable refis.   
- **Servicer compliance variance** (packet returns, response SLAs) is a recurring blocker across multiple firms.   
- Veterans **lack visibility** into VALERI/records needed for hearings—FOIA/record requests often incomplete. 

**Recommendations:**
- **P0 | Stand up an interim “Retention Options Hub”** with guided paths per status (trial, underwriting, post‑VASP), including written next‑best‑actions and a **case‑owner callback SLA (≤48h)**.  
- **P0 | Require a “Servicer Response Clock”** for packet returns (e.g., **≤14 days**) with escalation to VA liaison when breached.   
- **P1 | Create a “Records Fast‑Track”** for VALERI/decision artifacts needed for court—standard templates + tracked fulfillment within **5 business days**. 


## 5. Category 2: COE & Eligibility
**Mentions / Volume:** ~9.4% of analyzed calls.

**Description:**  
COE denials/delays are driven by **documentation mismatches** (DD214, Title 10 time, retirement points), **discharge character not reflected**, and **portal/vendor errors** blocking COE retrieval—even for veterans with service‑connected ratings. Consequences include **missed closings, homelessness risk,** and prolonged appeals. 

**Representative quotes or metrics:**
- “Denied for **no DD214 showing enough Title 10** service (1990–1992) despite combat decorations.”   
- “**100% P&T PTSD** veteran—COE denial pending **higher‑level review**, outcome decides whether the family keeps their home.”   
- “**COE office doesn’t see the appeal approval**; lender deadlines passed; we’re living in a camper.” 

**Impact:**  
**Task‑blocking at the start of the journey** (purchase/assumption/refi). Even small data defects (name mismatch, discharge status) produce outsized harm.

**Temporal or segment patterns:**
- **May–Jun 2025:** COE becomes primary monthly driver as VASP wanes; **expedited COE** needs surge near closing dates. 

**Critical Observations:**
- Veterans and lenders report **portal/vendor failures** retrieving COEs; tickets often **closed without resolution**.   
- **Discharge upgrades** and DFD‑like exceptions lack predictable timelines; communication gaps increase fallout. 

**Recommendations:**
- **P0 | “COE Fix‑Desk”**: dedicated cross‑functional queue to **correct identity/discharge data**, with a 72‑hour decision or documented exception path.   
- **P1 | Lender COE Health Dashboard**: publish vendor status/outage advisories + workarounds; keep lenders informed in real time. 


## 6. Category 3: Appraisal & Inspection / Property Condition
**Mentions / Volume:** ~7.3% of analyzed calls.

**Description:**  
Veterans report VA‑approved properties later found **unsafe or non‑compliant** (electrical hazards, foundation instability, mold), **missed MPR issues**, and **builder warranty disputes**; some appraisers allegedly **overstep scope** (e.g., structural engineer referrals) or **miss defects** that shift repair costs to buyers. 

**Representative quotes or metrics:**
- “**Exposed live wires** over heating duct; water pipes above junction box; repairs quoted **$5,000+** to meet code.”   
- “Gold‑fortified **DR Horton** home needs **$80,000** in foundation stabilization; builder avoiding warranty.”   
- “Appraiser required **structural engineer** a week before closing; **$1,000** bill; boundaries crossed between appraisal & inspection.” 

**Impact:**  
**Safety risk** post‑purchase; significant **unplanned expenditure**; erosion of trust in VA appraisal/MPR processes.

**Temporal or segment patterns:**  
Patterns appear **program‑wide**, not regional‑specific; many disputes mature **weeks to months after closing**. 

**Critical Observations:**
- Inspection/appraisal **role clarity** is weak; buyers conflate scope and **assume “VA passed” equals comprehensive safety**.   
- **Warranty documentation gaps** (e.g., missing 2‑10 Builders Warranty at closing) complicate remedy paths. 

**Recommendations:**
- **P0 | Appraisal Transparency Pack**: plain‑language MPR scope cards + “what’s not covered”; require buyer acknowledgment pre‑closing.   
- **P1 | Builder/Warranty Check**: standard closing checklist verifying warranty delivery & signatures; auto‑escalate if missing. 


## 7. Category 4: Short Sale / Deed‑in‑Lieu & Servicer Delays
**Mentions / Volume:** ~7.8% of analyzed calls.

**Description:**  
Callers attempting short sale or deed‑in‑lieu face **months‑long delays**, contradictory instructions, or **requests never sent to VA**, leading to missed buyer deadlines and **avoidable losses**. 

**Representative quotes or metrics:**
- “**Planet Home Lending** extended deed‑in‑lieu for **4+ months**; now short sale stuck—VA says **no request** was received.”   
- “Servicer **lost documents**; appraisal not released; closing dates repeatedly missed (July–Sept).” 

**Impact:**  
**Forced equity loss**, prolonged stress, and **increased foreclosure probability** when alternatives stall.

**Critical Observations:**
- Missing **submission proof**; veterans lack visibility into whether servicers **actually escalated to VA**. 

**Recommendations:**
- **P0 | “Alt‑Outcome Tracker”**: shared status page (short sale/deed‑in‑lieu) with **timestamped hand‑offs** (servicer→VA), visible to borrower. 


## 8. Category 5: Assumption & Entitlement Restoration
**Mentions / Volume:** ~1.9% of analyzed calls.

**Description:**  
Post‑divorce/transfer scenarios stall on **assumption packets**, lender **cash‑out pushes**, or refusal to honor **VA approvals**—blocking **entitlement restoration** and new purchases. 

**Representative quotes or metrics:**
- “Lender is **pushing cash‑out refinance** for a **non‑veteran ex‑spouse** instead of a proper assumption.”   
- “VA appeal **approved release of liability**, but **servicer imposed new post‑appeal conditions**, blocking closing.” 

**Impact:**  
**Eligibility lock‑up**; veterans cannot reuse benefits; legal/financial exposure persists.

**Recommendations:**
- **P1 | Assumption Playbook**: standardized packet, timelines (30–90 days), and **escalation when servicer overlays conflict** with VA approvals. 


## 9. Category 6: Communication & Courtesy
**Mentions / Volume:** ~1.4% of analyzed calls.

**Description:**  
Veterans repeatedly cite **robotic scripts, hang‑ups, ghosting** and low empathy—especially in high‑stakes foreclosure/COE moments—creating **secondary harm** (panic attacks, worsened PTSD). 

**Representative quotes:**
- “Agent talked for five minutes **without letting me speak**, explained foreclosure **condescendingly**; **panic attack** ensued.”   
- “**Hung up** after my questions; felt **cold‑shouldered** twice today.” 

**Recommendations:**
- **P0 | Courtesy Standard & Coaching**: empathy scripts, **no‑disconnect rule**, and supervisor backstop; publish **service SLAs** for callbacks.
