## **Research Goals**

1. Understand Veterans’ needs, expectations, and preferences for making payments, specifically a)financial management behaviors b)decision making around amounts and timelines c) interest and challenges in automatic/recurring payments

2. Test the usability of the Veteran’s overpayment debt experience in end to end flow testing (debt summary → details → resolve → choose payment option → Pay.gov HCP → confirmation)

## **Research Questions**

* What are Veterans’ current workflows and mental models for making payments on VA.gov?  
* What other comparable payment experiences do they prefer? Why?  
* What challenges or pain points do Veterans encounter in the current experience?  
* How easy is it to read and comprehend the pages?  
* Which aspects of the layout are most and least effective and why?  
* How does the experience compare to what they expected?  
* How confident and secure do they feel in the payment process? What creates confidence?  
* What factors influence Veterans’ decisions on payment amount/timeline (full vs partial vs plan)?  
* Do Veterans want recurring/auto-pay options? What trust/controls would they need?  
* Do Veterans notice/understand the transition and does it impact trust?

## **Methodology**

* Method 1: Semi-structured interviews \- appropriate for generative research  
* Method 2: Usability testing \- appropriate for evaluative research

## **Hypotheses and Conclusions**

* Veterans will prefer the more streamlined experience of hosted collection pages, including pre-populated account details and staying within the same browser  
  * **Likely true**  
* Veterans will be able to understand the difference between full and partial payment options and make a selection; if they hesitate or are unsure, we will identify what decision support (content, examples, explanations of consequences, or links to payment plans/financial help) is needed  
  * **Definitely true**  
* Veterans will feel confident and secure that their payment has has been received in the new experience  
  * **Likely true**  
* Veterans will want more diverse payment methods for submitting payments, including interest in recurring payments and autopay  
  * **Definitely true**

### **Finding 1: Most participants feel confused and upset by initial overpayment notifications**

Most participants feel upset and confused when they receive initial VA notification about their overpayment. Many are unaware they had done something to cause an overpayment and do not understand the specific reasons for incurring the overpayment, or how to fix potential processing errors that might have led to the overpayment. When they contact internal VA parties for more information, they feel frustrated and confused by how different parts of the VA don’t work together. Some avoid calling the VA due to long wait times in the past.

*\> “And so I have a lot of colleagues, too, who I hear troops that say, oh, they're so misinformed about how to make payments, and if you overpay, you don't want to be chasing down a debt that might get worked itself out.  like, I served 20 days, but the VA says I only served 10 days, so I still owe them more money, how do I pay them? .It's a big mystery, is what I hear a lot of, from my own experience and what people say, too.”*

*\-P2*

*\> “It kind of came out of left field, but, you know, so I owed something, I owed something, right? I was a little shocked, because I didn't understand it, but then when I read the letter, and I read it, like, 3 times, then I called the 800 number, and I was on the phone for, like, 20 minutes, and she explained it pretty well, so…”*

*\-P3*

*\> “So that letter comes first, and that comes from the regional office and that letter says, we have made a decision on your claim, blah blah blah blah. And then two weeks later is when that debt notification, what do you want to do with it? So there's two different parts of the VA, not really working together and it’s just crazy.”* 

*\-P4*

### **Finding 2: Most participants pay overpayments by actively or passively enrolling in a repayment plan that automatically deducts a portion of their monthly VA benefit payments; they do not pay these debts online**

Most participants make a passive or active decision to pay overpayments by enrolling in a repayment plan, in which a portion of their debt is automatically deducted from their monthly benefits. Only one participant in this study paid their overpayment online. After reading paper letters from the VA, they either decide to do nothing because it’s easier than having to call the VA directly, or they call a VA representative to set up the plan.

*“They're making us take money out of our paycheck, so I guess I kind of rationalized it like that. It’s one less stressor for me–it just means that my disability pay goes down for X amount of months while they do this, but I don't need to then pull money out of a budget to pay. So it just kind of becomes where you get to the point where you say, well, how much time and stress and energy and effort and what all are you going to take away from the amount of time you're probably gonna have to devote to this.”* 

*\-P17*

*\> “They ended up just siphoning out my benefits. I don’t know I feel like the VA they’re so slow sometimes, so I don’t think they purposely let it build up but I’ve heard from many veterans including myself that they’ll take their time and then hit you with a bill. I had to speak to somebody through VA customer service and I approved it. I picked the one where they would take the less amount of money out.”*

*\-P7* 

### **Finding 3: Most participants rely on simple tools, such as spreadsheets, bank accounts, and calendar reminders, to get a birds eye view of their finances, track and reconcile individual expenses, and manually set reminders to make “non-essential” payments to ensure sufficient funds.**

Most participants use simple tools to monitor spend and stay on top of accounting and reconciliation. Most keep a spreadsheet to track expenses and funds in a single location. They regularly look at their accounts online to check for errors or fraud on individual transactions. 

Many set account reminders and notifications to manually pay certain bills– these can be bills that fluctuate, such as credit cards or utilities, or bills that they cannot set to dates that align with pay days so they have sufficient funds to cover the transaction.

*\>“I had lost my job recently, I got laid off, so I was just trying to, like, you know, trying to, lower \[VA debt payments\] so I went through my savings, and see how much I'd get from my partner, just trying to see what's manageable I could do to contribute each month. We had a spreadsheet and everything, just calculate the bills of the house, you know, and other bills and everything, so that's how we calculate it.”*

*\-P7*

*\>I kind of have, like, an old-school Excel sheet budget, and stuff, like, I haven't quite found an app that does things super well, I think probably to the level of detail that I would like. I​​'m taking it in each month, like, know what kind of standard stuff needs to get paid for, like rent, utilities, car insurance, like, monthly recurring bill. And then I kind of track it through US Bank and USAA and stuff, so, they'll kind of tell you, like, general trends of, like, hey, this is what's coming in, this is what's generally going out. \[Then\] I’ll draw conclusions for next month and make adjustments as necessary on whatever disposable income that I have…*

*…My REI credit card I usually split out whenever I get paid and stuff, and just paying off, instead of just doing, like, one lump sum per month and stuff just makes it easier to do partial installments…that one I don't tend to do automatic payments for, and so just because I like to kind of look through everything. I just set up, kind of, reminders on my Google calendar”*

*\-P11*

*\>”I use USAA, and what I really like about them is you can actually go to reminders, and so you can put a reminder in there saying, my mortgage is coming out every month, even if it's being pulled and it's not scheduled and you can see a forecast of what your account looks like for the next two months.”*

### **Finding 4: Most participants strategically use financial tools and services, such as autopay, payment plans, and credit cards, to save time, earn rewards, and minimize interest. Most automate payments they believe are essential,such as rent and car payments**

Many participants strategically use financial services to save time and money. Most set up automatic payments for bills they prioritize as essential, such as rent and car payment, and time them with pay days so they don’t have to think about it. If finances allow, they pay bills automatically.  

Many use payment plans to make large purchases. They are highly sensitive to interest rates and switch plans for better rates, and they set up repayment timelines with the lowest amount of interest they can afford.

Some participants keep funds in high yield savings accounts for short periods before transferring to checking accounts to pay bills. Some use credit cards for specific purchases to get rewards and cashback. 

\> *“Auto insurance is not gonna change, my home insurance is not going to change, so that's kind of instantly \[on auto-pay\]..But I want to do those certain bills \[manually\] where it could be up and down \[like\] my electricity bill.”* 

*\-P7*

*\> “I bought a 1995 brand new Chevrolet Corvette. Initially, I had a different loan, but Navy Federal was going to save me $50 if I just signed up with them.  I think I chose the 5-year plan, because even though it was 5 years, I was paying more interest but the monthly payment was more manageable.”*

*\-P9*

*\> “I'm using SoFi, because it has a high savings yields. And then, when I'm ready, I'll transfer it from my SoFi to my Chase, because I'm trying to build a relationship with Chase, and then I'll just use that card to pay for \[my bill\].”*

### **Finding 5: Most participants express high confidence after completing the entire userflow. They don’t mind the VA.gov to Pay.gov transition and feel secure even if they don’t initially notice the site redirection.**

Many participants highlight several design elements that make the experience easy to scan and understand. They appreciate the clear icons, large and readable fonts, and bold due dates, all of which helped important information stand out quickly. They describe the experience as “straightforward,” “seamless,” “simple,” and “user-friendly.” Ten out of eleven participants rate their confidence completing the flow as a 10 on a scale of 1–10.). One participant rated their confidence at 6, attributing it to unresolved overpayment context questions rather than difficulty navigating the prototype.

\>”At no point would I have been like, I'm confused what this does, or where I'm supposed to click, or how to navigate through this.”

\-P11

\>"It was just set up so seamlessly, and very user-friendly. It was just a really great prototype, honestly." 

\-P13

\>"Everything that I expected, it popped up every single time."

\-P6

No participants report concerns with the transition from VA.gov to Pay.gov. They feel comfortable due to VA and government branding, seamless navigation, and expectation that government services often use third-party payment systems. Some participants notice the transition, citing  the “Continue to Pay.gov” button, while others do not notice the redirect. These participants have no concerns once the redirect was pointed out to them.

\>"The VA banner. That is good to reassure me that I'm still within a government URL."

\-P2

\>"The transition between the VA and Pay.gov, I like that it appeared to be seamless."

\-P4

\>"Doesn't feel that different, I mean, it has a .gov at the end of it, so I assume it to be secure."

### **Finding 6: Many participants point out critical information gaps in the experience. This decreases their ability to make confident decisions.**

Many participants want more information about partial payments and how to avoid negative collection actions sooner. Many participants believe that partial payment and payment plan information appeared too late in the flow or not at all. Participants who saw the full balance upfront may risk disengaging before discovering that partial payments were available.

Many participants are confused that a partial payment option does not appear upfront — they click through three screens before it appears as an option on the final payment screen. 

Many participants associate “collections” with credit damage and debt collectors. They are confused about when the overpayment would be sent to collections and what actions they could take to prevent collections other than paying the full amount. A few participants express confusion and concern about whether they need to pay while disputing the overpayment.

Some participants question why they need to see overpayment details like file number, payee number, and deduction code. The overpayment details section includes a bolded sentence, “You may need to provide the following details to resolve this debt,” followed by file number, payee number, person entitled, and deduction code, with a dropdown definition. Despite instructional framing, participants think these fields would only be relevant for phone interactions, not for completing a payment online. Some scroll past them quickly, and a few note the space would be better used for information they actually wanted but could not find, such as the specific reason for their debt or a payment history.

\>“If I do want to dispute it, then I probably would just pay the minimum amount. And then… that way, it shows I made a payment, and then go and dispute it.” 

\-P13

\>“I bet lots of people wouldn’t want to click this, because they’re like, I don’t have $830, so what’s the point?”.... At no point did it say to start paying off your balance, it just said, pay your balance, pay this debt, or you’re in trouble. If I pay a partial amount, is that okay, or am I gonna still be in trouble?.....  I guess it would vary from person to person, but I’m like, this is irrelevant. Like, these are irrelevant things to me, unless, like, I don’t know why I need that information… it says you may need this information to resolve the debt, but there’s no phone number up here to do that.”

\-P16

\>“Dispute, you have to continue making payments while they review the dispute. Okay, well, what were the monthly payments?”

\-P17

### **Finding 7: Many participants request more granular information about overpayments, including why it occurred and payment history–their absence created doubt.**

#### **Many participants request more granular information about overpayments, including why it occurred and payment history–their absence created doubt**

Many participants feel frustrated by VA not sharing more detail about individual overpayments and not understanding where they are within VA’s holistic debt resolution cycle. They wish to see the type of information about their debt they view as standard for financial services so that they can manage it as part of their existing accounting and reconciliation process.

The Debt Portal currently only shows the current balance and the amount originally owed. A feature that displayed balance increases and decreases was removed due to a bug on the data source being provided by an external team.

Many participants expected that payment made towards the debt would be shown on the site. Several participants described wanting to see their debt displayed the way a financial institution would display a loan with a clear breakdown of what was originally owed, what had been paid, and the current remaining balance. The lack of transparency on payments made caused concern enough to prevent some participants from making a payment.

#### Some participants appreciate the Overpayment Letter History and expect to view full letter content. Some say they might overlook or not open paper letters due to the volume of mail they receive from the VA. A few feel frustrated they can not access it from the screen.

#### **Some participants think the list of possible overpayment reasons is too generic – they expect specific information about why they have an overpayment. A few believe the VA intentionally withholds this information.**

\>“… but I think the one thing that’s missing is… now let’s say Hector goes and makes a payment for $50, and then he sees, like, his actual bill, but he doesn’t ever see, ok, confidence, to build confidence, like, yeah, they’ll receive my payment, apply it to my debt, and then I can just keep doing that every month. To see the debt coming down, it’s one payment, but then it goes into a black hole, and then you never really see to trust that, like, the payment that I made is actually… it’s being applied, but I want to see it.”

\-P2

*\> “I knew that at some point I’m going to be requested to overpay this because they’re using the wrong facts. And so I was like well, this doesn't seem right, but then I also was like, I don't really know how to contact people at the VA, like, that could really fix it, because it just seemed like a system issue. And so I was like, I'm just gonna wait for them to let me know.”* 

*\-P15*

*\> “ It'd be nice just to see a monthly, almost like an amortized, rate where you would see it less and less each month, and you would know how many more payments…. I mean, if you owe, don't you want to kind of know your balance? I mean, they're taking it out monthly. Even, like, in a bank account, right? Even when you're writing checks and stuff, you have a balance, and it shows… you get an itemized list of all the checks you wrote for that month, or all the deposits and all the withdrawals. It's a simple system to do it. Somebody has the records of it.”*

*\-P9*

\>“It could be just, I am not privy to, like, the reason why they can’t seem to attach a PDF of the letter, like, next to it in the overpayment letter history, but I think that would be particularly helpful.”

\-P11

### **Finding** 

*Emerging theme*: Some participants mentioned feeling betrayed or disrespected by institutions, including the VA as well as other companies, when they face obstacles to making payments or incur debt they perceive as unnecessary. Their military service is a testament to their discipline and commitment and they hold institutions serving them to a similar standard.

*\> “ \[If T-mobile\] would breakdown payments even more, that’d be nice. I expect to get that type of service for being not just a military veteran, but for how many years I've been with the company, I feel like they should probably honor something like that, or a valued customer.”*

*\-P6*

*\> “\[The VA\] they're awfully prompt in collecting a debt, but not so prompt in processing the paperwork that would have avoided the debt, so I felt kind in a way, it's kind of a betrayal, because it's really simple action.”*

*\-P4*

## **Recommendations**

**Recommendation: Provide transparency on the overpayment process, including guidance on what Veterans need to do to avoid negative actions and what those negative actions are.** Some participants expressed concern about the language, stating it felt more alarming than informative, especially for a Veteran who is just finding out about the overpayment. They understood "collection action" as the amount being sent to a third party collection agency. Other than paying the full amount due, there was a lack of information on setting up a payment plan and the amount and frequency of payments needed to prevent negative action.

* Create clear content describing when Veterans will incur late fees, interest rates (including highlighting when there is none), and timeline for debts being sent to treasury for collections, highlighting associated 20% interest charges. 

  This will communicate clear deadlines and consequential interest rate in a way that fits into a Veteran’s interest sensitive mental model, which will likely increase payment frequency and improve perception of VA efficiency through outlining clear process and guardrails to prevent unnecessary fees.

Supporting evidence: 7 participants mentioned this: P2, P4, P9, P11, P13, P16, P17

**Recommendation: Prioritize developing an online self-service repayment plan that enables Veterans to select amount and frequency to be withdrawn from their monthly benefits over time as well as the option to pay off in lump sum.** 

* Empowering Veterans with choice and increased autonomy over their finances will improve trust in the VA and day-to-day financial well-being.

Supporting evidence: 7 participants mentioned this: P2, P4, P6, P7, P13, P15, P16

**Recommendation: For overpayments that will be auto-deducted from benefits over time, include clear language describing how much will be deducted each month, original debt amount, when the deductions started, how much has been paid, and when they are projected to stop.** 

* This enables Veterans to plan VA income and upcoming expenses within their existing systems and tools, which will increase perception of VA transparency and create trust. 

Supporting evidence: 5 participants mentioned this: P2, P4, P9, P13, P16

**Recommendation: Display payment history and a running balance so Veterans can verify their payments are being applied.**

* Include payments made online, by phone, by mail, and through automatic benefit deductions so Veterans can verify their payments are being received and applied regardless of how they pay.

Supporting evidence: 4 participants mentioned this: P2, P9, P16, and P17

**Recommendation: Work with DMC and VBA to improve debt notification letters.**

* Revise initial debt letter content by outlining clear next steps and time frames for each stage of the debt resolution process. 

  Acknowledge the time it takes to consolidate information across multiple VA departments and express gratitude for Veteran patience. Use language that shows appreciation and care for a Veteran who might be upset and confused when receiving the letter. 

  These trauma informed changes will improve the Veteran’s emotional well-being and trust for the VA and increase Veteran follow-through on next steps.   

Supporting evidence: 8 participants mentioned this: P2, P3, P4, P6, P11, P13, P16, P17

**Recommendation: Provide the specific reason the overpayment exists, rather than a generic list of possible causes.**

Supporting evidence: 5 participants mentioned this: P2, P11, P15, P16, and P17

**Recommendation: Present context on why Overpayment details (file number, deduction code, etc) are being shown and when Veterans would need them**

Supporting evidence: 5 participants mentioned this: P4, P9, P11, P13, P16

**Recommendation: Allow Veterans to view and download their overpayment letters directly from the Debt Portal.**

Supporting evidence: 3 participants mentioned this: P11, P16, and P17

### **Desired User Outcome**

Outcomes:

This research supports our desired user outcome by:

* **Determine how to iterate VA.gov pages to improve the payment experience for Veterans prior to build and release**  
  This research identified specific, actionable improvements that can be made on the Debt Portal before launch. Improving the content and adding features to provide more transparency will improve Veteran’s payment experience.

* **Advance user-centered digital strategy for delivering future payment features and functionality**  
  This research surfaced unmet Veteran needs that included transparency into the reason for the overpayment and overall resolution process, payments made, and payment plans.

* **Gain foundational understanding of Veteran payment needs and preferred methods**  
  Across 11 participants, this research documented how Veterans track their finances, what information they need to feel confident making a payment, and what comparable experiences they use as mental models for what the VA experience should provide.

* **Decrease rate of drop off due to Veteran frustration with manual data entry**  
  The experience pre-populates account information on the backend which prevents the need for manual data entry. The streamlined experience was well received.

* **Increase online payment transactions and decrease analog payment transactions**  
  Several participants who had previously set up a payment plan over the phone said they would use the prototype to pay online. Removing the barriers identified in this research, particularly around payment plan setup and the ability to deduct payment from their benefits will shift more transactions from analog to online channels.

* **Evaluate trust/confidence during the VA.gov \--\> Pay.gov transition and at confirmation**  
  10 of 11 participants rated their overall confidence at 10/10. The VA.gov to Pay.gov transition did not create trust or security concerns.

### **Desired Business Outcome**

This research supports our desired business outcome by:

* **Overpayment Debt Payments are successful being processed via new pay.gov hosted collection page**

  ​​This research validated that participants were able to navigate the full end-to-end payment flow without significant confusion or abandonment. 

* **Reduced call center calls related to payment questions or issues.**

  This research identified the specific gaps that currently drive Veterans to call the DMC, including not knowing the reason for their debt, not being able to see payment history, the contents of debt letters, and understanding how to avoid negative actions. In our prototype, participants were not able to understand how to initiate a payment plan online, and not knowing what happens after making a partial payment.  
     
* **Enhanced data visibility and reporting on Veteran payment behavior.**

  By documenting how Veterans navigate the payment flow, where they slow down, what information they look for, and which resolution options they are unaware of, this research establishes a behavioral baseline that can be used to interpret analytics data after launch and identify where further iteration is needed.

* **Improved Veteran satisfaction and retention for the debt management portal.**

  The research highlighted what worked well, the experience was easy to navigate and that the visual hierarchy was effective. It also identified key gaps to address before launch, including the need for greater transparency around the cause of the overpayment and its lifecycle, as well as the need for additional payment options.

## **Next Steps**

* Work with PO and DMC to understand the overpayment process from notifications to the collections process, including what penalties are involved, at what point collection actions are triggered, and whether disputing or requesting financial help pauses collection activity.

* Collaborate with the Content team to provide plain-language information on where the Veteran is in the overpayment collection process as well as the specific consequences that apply to each Veteran's situation. Provide guidance on what negative consequences apply, when they are triggered, and how each resolution option (partial payment, payment plan, waiver, dispute) affects the Veteran's standing.

* In the short term, provide guidance and a link to the unauthenticated experience (pay.va.gov) in order for Veterans to set up a payment plan. Provide information on how they can pay via benefit reduction.

* Long term, include a payment plan option within Pay.gov Hosted Collections. Allow Veterans to set up a payment plan with their benefits. Allow Veterans to edit their existing payment plans online.

* Explore whether Veterans' specific reason for their overpayment and timeline of overpayments can be surfaced on the overpayment detail page.

* Display payment history feature (dependent on external team).

* Display letters referenced in the letter history section as PDFs (dependent on external team). Explore surfacing the basic content of the letters if possible.

* Conduct competitive analysis study to gain further insight on the financial services companies participants like to use \- this will provide inspiration for updating designs and identifying new opportunities
