# VA Mobile App Review Analysis
## Q2 FY26 App Review & Feedback (Jan–Mar 2026)

---

## Analysis Motivation & Approach

**VA Mobile App Analysis | Q2 FY26**

### Why We Conducted This Review

To summarize Veterans’ experiences with the VA Health & Benefits mobile app across **Q2 FY26 (January–March 2026)** by rolling up monthly app feedback. This quarter’s synthesis draws on the January, February, and March 2026 user feedback reports so we can see stable themes, month-to-month shifts, and priorities for the next cycle.

### What We Wanted to Learn

- What aspects of the app did Veterans consistently value across the quarter?
- Which pain points persisted or intensified (especially sign-in, appointments, prescriptions, messaging)?
- Where did February’s broader instrumentation (in-app surveys) and March’s email channel add nuance to store-only trends?

### What Are We Hoping to Achieve?

- Prioritize improvements that address the highest-volume and highest-impact friction from Q2 FY26.
- Align roadmap and research with recurring Veteran motivations (reliable access, complete information, end-to-end tasks).

### What data did we look into?

- **Apple App Store and Google Play Store** reviews with star ratings, summarized monthly (January: 331; February: 343; March: 347 store responses; **1,021** total store ratings in the quarter).
- **February 2026 only:** App Profile “Give Feedback” survey, Prescription Refill intercept survey, and monthly health metrics (quantitative; see February monthly file).
- **March 2026:** **Mobile app email inbox** qualitative messages (6 in-period), integrated in the March monthly file alongside store reviews (not star-rated).

### How did we analyze these data?

- Rollup of themes, feature-level notes, and distributions from the **January 2026**, **February 2026**, and **March 2026 User Feedback** markdown analyses.
- Cross-month comparison of store rating mix and recurring qualitative themes.

## Learnings from Q2 FY26

---

## At-a-Glance

**VA Mobile App Analysis | Q2 FY26**

### Veteran satisfaction stayed strong in store reviews, with roughly three-quarters of ratings positive across the quarter.

Combined App Store and Google Play data for Jan–Mar 2026 show **~77%** of ratings at 4–5 stars (see Data Snapshot). February’s store cohort had the highest share of positive ratings (**80.2%**); January and March were slightly lower but in line with each other (**~75–77%**).

### Sign-in and authentication remained the dominant pain point across all three months.

Monthly analyses repeatedly cited ID.me / Login.gov loops, return to the sign-in screen after verification, unreliable biometrics, and post-update login failures. March email feedback echoed the same patterns, including multi-month Android cases and a cross-device contrast (Android loop vs. iPad working).

### Appointment completeness and prescription visibility drew sustained criticism; messaging and “full journey” gaps grew in specificity.

Veterans asked for purpose of visit, provider, clinic, and room details; many wanted booking or rescheduling in the app. Prescriptions stayed popular when they worked, but **February and March** showed more pressure on refill/renewal workflows, missing or “not available” fill dates, and loss of **tracking** visibility after updates. Messaging issues expanded to stale threads vs. web, save/send confusion, and delete/trash expectations. February introduced **medical supplies** (e.g., hearing aids) as a new feature ask alongside prescriptions.

## Positive Sentiments

**VA Mobile App Analysis | Q2 FY26**

### Veterans consistently praised convenience, consolidation of VA tasks, and ease of use when the app worked as expected.

Across January–March, positive store reviews described the app as a **single place** for appointments, prescriptions, secure messaging, travel claims, and benefits information—often favorable vs. VA.gov or phone-only workflows.

> "This is without a doubt the best app I have ever used. It is so easy to use and helpful and extremely thoughtful all the information that I need in one place thank you everyone for helping us veterans." — March 2026 (store)

> "I have been using the VA web interface for years and they were slow and difficult to navigate. I just tried VA app and I will never use the web interface again." — February 2026 (store)

> "It's a wonderful resource for veterans! The app provides convenience and is easy to navigate with tools for communicating with your medical team, verifying appointments and even requesting refills for your medications" — January 2026 (store)

---

## Negative Sentiments

**VA Mobile App Analysis | Q2 FY26**

### Frustration concentrated on authentication loops, post-update reliability, and “incomplete” journeys—especially appointments and prescriptions.

Veterans reported being returned to sign-in after ID.me verification, apps that “won’t open,” or features that **stopped working after an update**. Negative threads also targeted **missing appointment context** (purpose, provider, clinic), **inability to book/reschedule** in-app, and **prescription** data missing or unreliable (including refill dates and tracking in March).

> "Still unusable! … I log in to the app, now it asks me to verify if I want to go to VA.gov and when I hit continue… it takes me right back to the apps sign in." — February 2026 (store)

> "The latest updates have wiped out refill dates, appointments, and a slew of medically needed information from the app." — March 2026 (store)

> "This is app fail to log in, fail to recognize ID me log in and my VA log in passwords…" — January 2026 (store)

## Q2 FY26 Comparison to Previous Quarters

**VA Mobile App Analysis | Q2 FY26**

| Metric | Q1 FY26 (Oct–Dec 2025) | Q2 FY26 (Jan–Mar 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Store positive sentiment (app analysis) | ~75.2% | ~77.3% (combined Jan–Mar store rollup) |
| Dominant pain point | Sign-in | Sign-in (persistent) |
| Prescriptions | Strong feature in prior quarterly narrative | **Mixed:** strong praise but **Feb–Mar** regressions on visibility, fill dates, tracking |
| Messaging | Ongoing provider/thread issues | Added emphasis on **web parity**, **compose UX**, **stale threads** (March) |
| New/emerging asks | — | **Medical supplies** ordering (Feb); **travel pay setup** + **location** troubleshooting (Mar email); **payment/copay** in app (recurring) |

### Key trends across Q2 FY26 (Jan–Mar)

1. **Sign-in is still the #1 cross-cutting friction**—store and March email reinforce loops and device-specific behavior.
2. **Prescriptions** remained a **hero** feature in narrative volume but **regression sensitivity** rose in late Q2 (dates/tracking).
3. **Appointment information density** and **in-app scheduling actions** are recurring, growing sharper in February (reschedule).
4. **Messaging** quality issues are **technical and UX** (sync, send, drafts), not only “provider list” issues.
5. **Travel pay** split between **delighters** and **strugglers**; **setup/onboarding** surfaced as a concrete gap in email.
6. **February surveys** (Give Feedback, Rx intercept) skew **positive at scale**—use alongside **store** for sentiment, not as replacement for qualitative themes.

## Recommendations for Q3 FY26

**VA Mobile App Analysis | Q2 FY26**

### Priorities carried forward

- **Sign-in stabilization** and cross-platform verification of return paths after ID.me / VA.gov handoff.
- **Appointment detail** surfacing (provider, purpose, clinic) and **scheduling/rescheduling** exploration where systems and policy allow.
- **Prescription** display and **release regression** discipline—especially fill dates, tracking, and renewal/refill edge cases.
- **Messaging** parity with web and compose affordances (draft vs. send, thread management expectations).

### Additional Q2 FY26-informed recommendations

1. **Regression suite for prescriptions:** Treat refill date, fill date, and **tracking** visibility as **release-blocking checks** after app or API changes (supported by March store + email).
2. **Travel pay onboarding:** Add concise **first-time setup** guidance (and consider in-app entry points) reflecting March email and persistent store findability themes.
3. **Messaging sync investigation:** Prioritize **stale thread** reports where app lags web (March)—triage as reliability, not cosmetic.
4. **Supplies pathway discovery:** Treat **hearing aid / CPAP supplies** asks (February) as a **product discovery** input alongside pharmacy stakeholders.
5. **Payments / copays:** Continue evaluating **in-app or deep-linked** paths for bills and copays where scope allows (recurring Jan–Mar).
