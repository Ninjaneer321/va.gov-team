
## **Conversation Guide (April 7-14, during cutover and early recovery)**


### **Moderator logistics**

Logistics to confirm periodically

Check Settings -> Meeting -> In Meeting (Advanced) > Automated captions, Full transcript, Save captions are all ON

Check Settings -> Meeting -> In Meeting (Basic) > Sound notification when someone joins or leaves is OFF

Check Settings -> Meeting -> In Meeting (Basic) > Screen sharing > Who can share > All Participants

Check Settings -> Meeting -> In Meeting (Basic) > Hide participant profile pictures in a meeting ON

2-3 days before the session

Confirm notetaker availability

Let notetaker know if anything has changed in the conversation guide, especially any new system statuses or changes to the transition timeline

Day of the session

Use #feedback-backchannel in Slack for real-time feedback from observers.

Mute your Slack notifications.

Send out the observer instructions to your observers.

Sign into Zoom website via Google as Perigean w/ credentials provided and start Zoom meeting 5-10 minutes before

Starting the session

In the Attendees section, make sure everyone except the participant is on mute.

In the Participant "more" menu, make sure "Play Join and Leave Sound" is unchecked

Check your screensharing setup and share permissions for participants.

When the participant is ready, begin the session with the following intro.

### **1. Intro + consent (3-5 minutes)**

Moderator script (adapt as needed):

Thanks for joining today. My name is [NAME]. I work on the team that helps improve VA.gov and related VA health tools.

Today we're going to talk about what you've experienced during the recent Oracle Health transition—especially with appointments, messages, medications, and medical records. We're not testing you—if anything feels confusing or frustrating, that's helpful feedback for us.

Participation is voluntary and will not impact your benefits.

In this session, I'm going to ask you to login to your VA.gov account and share your screen while you check a few things. This means that we may see some of your personal information.

Knowing this, are you comfortable if I record my screen and audio as we talk today? All videos are kept in a secure location and destroyed after analysis.

Are you comfortable with it if we share anonymized video clips with our VA colleagues for research purposes, provided we remove any information that can be used to identify you?

(If observers are present)

We also have a couple teammates listening in to help us take notes. Are you okay with that?

If no: Use Slack to kindly ask the observers to leave the Zoom.

If yes: Admit observers.

Then, ask the notetaker and observers to give a brief, camera-on introduction, like their first name and position with VA.gov. (This is meant to humanize the interaction of observation for the participant.)

Once complete, ask the notetaker and observers to turn off their cameras for the remainder of the session to save bandwidth.

If yes: Once I start recording, I am going to confirm that you are okay with me recording this session once more.

*Start recording and capture consent*

I have started recording. I'd like to confirm: Are you comfortable if I record my screen and the audio as we talk today?

Are you comfortable if we decide to use a recorded clip from our discussion today to share with other VA partners if we remove any information that can be used to identify you?

### **2. Warm-up: recent healthcare activity (3-4 minutes)**

Since we last spoke, what VA health tasks have you done?

Probes (check all that apply):



* Scheduled, changed, or canceled an appointment
* Sent or read secure messages with your doctor or care team
* Requested prescription refills or checked shipment status
* Viewed lab results or medical records

Which tools did you use?

Probes:



* VA.gov web, VA Health & Benefits app, My HealtheVet, email, text, phone, in-person

Follow-up probe (based on Check-in 1):

When you needed to do these tasks, did you try online first, or did you decide to call right away? Why?


---


### **3. Unprompted recall: what they noticed (4-5 minutes)**

Goal: capture what they noticed on their own before directing attention to alerts.

Without logging in yet, what, if any, changes have you noticed in the past week or so?

Did anything surprise you or feel unclear?

Did you receive any communications outside the site or app?

Probes:



* Email, text, facility phone call, town hall, letter, poster, or other

Did you notice any dates or deadlines? Were they easy to keep track of?

Did you feel like you understood *why* these changes were happening?


---


### **4. Screen share walkthrough (12-14 minutes total)**

Transition to screenshare:

"Please share your screen and log in the way you normally do. As you go, please think out loud about what you're seeing."


#### **4a) Medications (start here; highest risk/attention)**

*Once they're on the medications page: Tell me what you are looking at. What do you think you can do here today?*

Probe: do they notice any alerts?

Were you able to request refills online during the past week?

Were you able to track shipments online?

If they don't mention a notice after ~20 seconds:

"As you look at this page, is there anything here that looks like an update or notice?"

Key probes:



* If meds were blocked on or around April 8: what did you do instead?
* Did you refill earlier because of concerns about the transition? How did you decide when to refill?
* If you needed a medication urgently during the downtime, what was your plan?
* If you called the pharmacy: what happened? (business hours, voicemail, response time, how long until you heard back?)
* Do you see medications labeled "transferred"?
    * What does "transferred" mean to you?
    * Does seeing that label increase or reduce your trust?
* Do you see a combined medication list with new Rx numbers?
    * Do you recognize your medications?
    * Do the new Rx numbers create any confusion?
* What would you change about how this information is presented?


#### **4b) Secure Messaging**

*Once they're on secure messaging: Tell me what you are looking at. What do you think you can do here today?*

Probe: do they notice any alerts?

Were you able to send or read messages during the transition window?

If not, what did you do instead?

If messaging is now working: What feels different about it now compared to before?

Do you see anything that says "Oracle Health" or references "message pools"? If so, what does that mean to you?

If they see an alert or notice: On first read, what does it tell you? What action does it suggest? How would you want it worded differently?

Key probes (from Check-in 1 findings):



* Did you realize you could expand or click the alert to see more details?
* What would make it obvious that the alert is expandable or interactive?
* What would you change about how this update was communicated?


#### **4c) Appointments**

*Once they're on appointments: Tell me what you are looking at. What do you think you can do here today?*

Probe: do they notice any alerts?

Were you prevented from scheduling or changing appointments online at any point during the transition?

Do you see appointments from both Oracle Health and the old system together?



* Does it look complete and trustworthy?
* Do you see any duplicates, missing appointments, or confusing labels?

Key probes:



* If you needed to cancel or change an appointment during the downtime, were you able to do it? How?
* What would you change about how your appointments are displayed?


#### **4d) Medical Records + lab/test results notifications**

*Once they're on medical records: Tell me what you are looking at.*

Do you see any notices about delays or updates?

Are records "starting to come through"?



* Do you see what you expected to see?
* Is anything missing or confusing?

Have you received notifications for new lab or test results over the past week?



* Where did you receive them (email, text, app notification, VA.gov)?
* Were they timely? Helpful? Too many? Not enough?

Key probes (from Check-in 1 findings):



* If you see "may be a delay," ask: "How long do you assume the delay is?" and "What would you do if you needed the information urgently?"
* What would you change about how delays are communicated?


---


### **5. Reflection + synthesis (5-6 minutes)**


#### **5a) Overall experience: helpfulness, trust, control**

Thinking about your experience this past week, how would you describe it?

Probes:



* Helpful vs. frustrating
* Trustworthy vs. doubtful
* In control vs. helpless

Did your trust in VA digital tools change at all during the transition? If so, how?


#### **5b) Timing, frequency, and channels**

Were you notified at the right time?

Which channel worked best for you (email, text, app alert, in-person, phone)? Which worked worst?

Did you feel there were too many notices? Or not enough?


#### **5c) Prioritization: must-have improvements**

If you could change ONE thing about the alerts or communications, what would it be?

Probes (if needed):



* Clarity of wording (especially terms like "transferred," "Oracle Health," "may be a delay")
* Visibility or placement of alerts
* Phone numbers and after-hours support information
* A consolidated view of what's working and what's not
* Personalization to your specific facility and clinics
* Timing of when you received alerts

What would make you more likely to take action immediately when you see an alert or notice?

What would you want a friend or family member Veteran to know about this transition?


---


### **6. Consent to use video clips (1 minute)**

I want to circle back and re-confirm that you are comfortable if we were to share a video clip of this session with our VA partners.

Just to remind you, we will remove all information that could be used to identify you before sharing, and we'll only share the videos with VA partners with the purpose of improving VA.gov and other digital experiences.

If yes: Is there any part of our discussion you would NOT want shared in a video clip?


---


### **7. Thank-you and closing (1 minute)**

We really appreciate you taking the time to share your thoughts with us today. Your feedback is so helpful to us as we continue to work on the site and make sure it works for Veterans.

Thanks! Perigean will be sending you a thank you note with information about other research opportunities if you're interested in participating in future studies.

Thank you so much again, and enjoy the rest of your day!
