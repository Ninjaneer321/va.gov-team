# Notetaker and Observer Top 3 Callouts

Main takeaways from the research sessions

### P01

**UX Notetaker:**
- Participant uses the term claim as a "catch-all for VA-stuff". Used claim to refer to the CHAMPVA application multiple times
- Mentioned 3 times that it takes 2-3 weeks to receive mail (our time estimate of 7-10 business days to receive mail won't be accurate for people who live in rural areas, like this participant does)
- Overall positive impression of the prototype, mentioned that it's better than the current experience where they "have to dig"

**Observer 1:**
- Seemed like a great experience overall, and the participant was pleased with the entire process.
- We could still use some clarity in how we refer to "forms," "applications," and "claims." Not just on My VA, but sitewide.
- Overall, new My VA page was very helpful and informative.

**Observer 2:**
- Note for you all for later: He said he was confident in each person's app status but during the session once he uploaded a doc for one person, he noticed an info alert for spouse confirming an unrelated status.

### P02

**UX Notetaker:**
- Trying to find a completed claim requires them to dig
- Expectations around email updates and being able to find letters within CST. (not just the physical mailed letter)
- Naturally explored the CST and was able to find what they needed for tasks to find status or upload documents.

**Observer 1:**
- Scrolled past H1 and intro content. Didn't seem to notice it or have an impression of it positively or negatively.
- Initially expected update on closed claim on main view of Status tab in CST. Had to "search" for closed claim. (A reminder that Veterans have their current/most recent task in mind when they visit the site.)
- Found the step accordion content helpful, particularly where it noted the evidence request process.

**Observer 2:**
- They took great importance in accuracy and transparency around dates of status changes, and expected timelines, even if they were approximate
- They did not seem overwhelmed by the amount of information on the screens (I would expect otherwise: it's always nice to be proven wrong by a usability test! Maybe this was attributed to clear headings :clap: )
- They seemed to expect a continuation of information about the same application to appear not just in the same screen, but in the same location on the screen

### P03
**UX Notetaker:**
- Participant wants email notifications. Can take weeks to see mail if they're traveling. Mentions of sending things by mail or fax confirm their perception that the VA is outdated
- Wants to know everything up front: clear timelines for each step, updates on enrollment to be surfaced to the "first page" (surfacing a newly closed app to MyVA in this case)
- Was expecting separate status for each applicant, but that's probably because they didn't fill out this fake application themselves - was going off of their previous experience of filing for one person at a time

**UX Lead:**
- User desired per person status.
- User desired more up front status on MYVA page.
- Find immediate access to the info easily and was able to upload docs.
- Wants electronic updates not hardcopy mail.

### P04
**UX Notetaker:**
- Was able to relatively easily navigate throughout the prototype
- Would like email alerts for status changes.
- Wants to be able to access the letters so he can start to take action quickly
- Wants multiple document upload functionality.

**UX Lead:**
- user had no problems with understanding the information, structure, and overall process
- User desires access to electronic versions of the mailed correspondence so immediate action can be taken and not have to wait for mail to arrive.
- User desires notification alerts what there are status changes - that will drive them back to VA.gov to review where things are and take action if something is needed.

**Observer 1:**
- Interesting slip: On My VA screen, P4 called the "In-progress forms" section "In-process forms" 3x during the first 2 scenarios.
- CST landing page: P4 initially wanted the segmented tab to default to "Closed" before realizing the “In progress” tab would not show all the the in-progress stuff. After noticing this, they liked the segmented tab options and thought it made sense.
- Wish list: P4 wants digital versions of the decision letters available in the tool and email notifications to get updates.

**Moderator:**
- The participant wanted the segmented tab to default to "Closed" before realizing the Claim and application status section on MyVA would not show all the in-progress claims. (I think at first they interpreted the Claim and application status section on MyVA as the place for all in-progress claims, and the CST home page as the archive of all claims)

### P06
**UX Notetaker:**
- They were able to navigate through the task as expected, except when it came to finding the final decision. The information was essentially hidden.
- They like consistency and became worried when things changed, especially if additional information wasn't surfaced. (don't make we wait for the letter about my son, i want to know why now, so i can take action)
- Was focused on taking action, and didn't see Jane's status until later.
- The queue was super interesting as well, i'm curious if that is relevant now since the backlog has gone down.

**UX Lead:**
- User wants more consistency with information  being organized on the first page. Along with stickiness of that info for a period of time.
- User want access to the letter immediately to take action and not wait. Produces high stress and anxiety - which will ultimately drive a support call.
- User doesn't want to re-provide info for dependents that has already been provided to get benefits / disability comp. All this info such as birth certificates and marriage licenses has been provided to get the disability rating.
- User desires more of a realtime number counter showing that the application is moving along. As long as they are seeing progression, they are reassured.

**Observer 1:**

**Observer 2:**
- Very passionate about moving away from paper, they used fax to submit their champva app and felt like it was an awful experience
- They really want to know where they're at in the 'queue'
- Often times said they would just call the phone number--especially if they saw 'ineligible' and no further detail for their child
- Ultimately happy with the prototype, despite a lot of feedback and passionate opinions regarding champva process



### P08
**UX Notetaker:**
- User wanted to be able to see all documents and related information that had been submitted 
- User like the partial decision, especially the transparency in the tool. You might receive a letter and then ask, where are the rest of the letters. 
- User struggled to find the CHAMPVA card once it was no longer visible on MyVA. Stopped before going into the card since they felt they had all the information they needed.

**UX Lead:**
- User wants a collective list of all CHAMPVA related content. Decision letters, upload files, etc. a comprehensive view of a "closed" file.
- User was unsuccessful in finding that John does was denied and struggle with the tasks when they didn see a card or champva related content for task 3
- User liked that on submissions they system knew who the submission was for and they didn't have to select the applicant.
