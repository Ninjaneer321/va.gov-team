## Session Summaries and Observer Top 3 Callouts

Main takeaway from the research sessions

### P01

**UX Notetaker:**
-

**Observer 1:**
- Seemed like a great experience overall, and the participant was pleased with the entire process.
- We could still use some clarity in how we refer to "forms," "applications," and "claims." Not just on My VA, but sitewide.
- Overall, new My VA page was very helpful and informative.

**Observer 2:**
- Note for you all for later: He said he was confident in each person's app status but during the session once he uploaded a doc for one person, he noticed an info alert for spouse confirming an unrelated status.

### P02

**UX Notetaker:**
- Trying to find a completed claim requires them to dig
- Expectations around email updates and being able to find letters within CST. (not just the physical mailed letter)
- Naturally explored the CST and was able to find what they needed for tasks to find status or upload documents.

**Observer 1:**
- Scrolled past H1 and intro content. Didn't seem to notice it or have an impression of it positively or negatively.
- Initially expected update on closed claim on main view of Status tab in CST. Had to "search" for closed claim. (A reminder that Veterans have their current/most recent task in mind when they visit the site.)
- Found the step accordion content helpful, particularly where it noted the evidence request process.

**Observer 2:**
- They took great importance in accuracy and transparency around dates of status changes, and expected timelines, even if they were approximate
- They did not seem overwhelmed by the amount of information on the screens (I would expect otherwise: it's always nice to be proven wrong by a usability test! Maybe this was attributed to clear headings :clap: )
- They seemed to expect a continuation of information about the same application to appear not just in the same screen, but in the same location on the screen
