# CHAMPVA Application Status MVP — Comprehensive UX Research Analysis

## Executive Summary

1. **The status tool concept is strongly validated** — Most participants found the prototype easy to use, with 7 of 9 rating ease-of-use a 5/5. File upload was universally praised as simple and intuitive.
2. **Finding a closed/decided application is the #1 usability failure** — All 9 participants struggled to find their CHAMPVA application once it moved to the "Closed" tab, with many experiencing anxiety/panic when it disappeared from MyVA.
3. **Participants universally demand email/text notifications and timelines** — Every single participant requested proactive digital notifications and estimated processing times. Reliance on physical mail is a major pain point.
4. **The "Request to add or remove a dependent" card causes confusion** — Multiple participants (P02, P06, P07, P09) mistook this card for their CHAMPVA application, disrupting task completion in Scenario 3.
5. **Participants want immediate access to denial reasons and decision letters** — When John Doe Jr. was deemed ineligible, participants expressed frustration at not being able to see *why*, driving them to call the VA.

## 9. OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION

### Quick Wins (Low Effort, High Impact)
1. **Add a "Recently decided" indicator on MyVA** — Show closed CHAMPVA applications on MyVA for 30-60 days after decision
2. **Rename "Closed" to "Completed" or "Decided"** — Reduce anxiety around the label
3. **Change "Change file" to "Choose file" or "Attach file"** — Matches user expectations
4. **Make the segmented tabs more visually interactive** — Add borders/button-like styling for mobile (P11)
5. **Add "Action needed" as a clickable link** — Multiple participants tried to click the tag

### Strategic Improvements (High Effort, High Impact)
6. **Email/text notifications for status changes** — Universal request; would reduce call center volume
7. **Digital access to decision letters** — Inline or linked in the status tool; eliminates mail wait
8. **Add estimated timelines per step** — Even approximate ranges would reduce anxiety
9. **Per-beneficiary step tracking** — Show where each person is independently in the process
10. **Explain ineligibility reasons inline** — Don't force users to wait for a mailed letter

### Feature Ideas
- **"Track claims" link on MyVA should include a "new decisions" badge**
- **Notification preferences during application submission** (opt-in to email/text)
- **Comprehensive file history** — Show all submitted and received documents in one view
- **Queue position indicator** (if feasible)

### Content/Messaging Opportunities
- Clarify the partial decision alert: *"after you submit more information"* is confusing after submission is complete (P11)
- The "review request" phrasing is odd — consider "Submit requested document" (P09)
- Add clarity about what "Step 1 of 4" means on the MyVA card without requiring navigation

## 10. GAPS & FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS

### What We Still Don't Know
- How **family member/beneficiary** participants (non-Veterans) would navigate the tool — all 9 participants were Veterans
- How participants would react to **real** notifications (email/text) vs. the concept
- Whether the **"Request to add or remove a dependent"** confusion is specific to the prototype or would persist in production
- How participants handle **multiple simultaneous applications** across different benefit types
- What is the **acceptable frequency** for notifications — every change or batched?

### Questions for Follow-Up Research
1. What specific information should be included in email/text notifications?
2. How should the system handle the transition from "in progress" to "decided" on MyVA? (time-based? persistent link?)
3. Would a separate "CHAMPVA" section on MyVA test better than inclusion in "Claims and application status"?
4. What is the minimum viable information for an inline denial explanation?
5. How do family members who are the actual beneficiaries experience this tool?

### Assumptions to Validate
- That decision letters can be digitized and served through the status tool
- That processing time estimates are feasible and wouldn't create false expectations
- That email notifications won't generate additional confusion or security concerns
- That the "Closed" → "Completed" rename wouldn't conflict with other VA.gov patterns

### Areas Needing Quantitative Validation
- Task completion time for finding a closed application (to measure improvement after redesign)
- Call center volume correlation — does tool usage reduce CHAMPVA-related calls?
- Time-to-action on evidence requests: digital upload vs. mail response

## Risk Factors & Concerns

1. **Call center overload risk** — Without digital notification and inline denial info, this tool may *increase* call volume as users discover decisions but can't act on them
2. **Accessibility on mobile** — Filter tabs and small links were not discoverable for mobile users; may fail WCAG interactive element requirements
3. **Anxiety amplification** — The disappearing card creates panic; this could damage trust in VA.gov as a reliable source of truth
4. **Terminology debt** — "Claims," "applications," "forms," and "completed" are used inconsistently across MyVA and CST; creates cognitive load

>[!NOTE]
> - _I don't think 2 is true?_

## Methodological Limitations

- **All participants were Veterans**, not family members/beneficiaries — the primary CHAMPVA users
- **Prototype limitations** meant participants couldn't actually select files, which confused some during upload
- **Participants didn't fill out the original application**, so multi-beneficiary confusion may be overstated
- **Small sample size** (n=9) — patterns are directional, not statistically significant
- **No-show bias** — P05, P10, P12+ participants had minimal/template notes, suggesting high attrition
- **Observer effect** — Moderator redirection during Scenario 3 means "unassisted" completion rates for finding the closed app are truly 0/9

## Contradictions in the Data

- P01 rated 5/5/5 but expressed confusion about partial decisions and uses "claim" incorrectly
- P06 rated the tool 5 for ease of use but experienced significant anxiety and frustration during Scenario 3
- Several participants said "nothing confusing" in post-task but required redirection during tasks
