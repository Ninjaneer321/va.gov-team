As Task Manager moves beyond baseline parity CMT functionality. The team seeks to better understand what features and functions will be most useful for current and prospective task manager users.

The research aims to:

`1.` Gather insight into the types of workflows, use cases and scenarios clinicians are hoping to manage through Task Manager.

`2.` Gather insights into the user task management workflows from task creation, to assignment, to task completion.

`3.` Gather feedback from current task manager users regarding pain points, bright spots and desires relating to task creation, monitoring and follow-through.

#### Task assignment:

**Hypothesis:** The current object model (relationship between tasks, teams and members) may not meet the needs of all teams using task manager.

- `Definitely True`– While some clinicians tack standalone tasks, tracking multiple tasks per patient, 21 of 24 clinician research participants maintain 1 task per patient, with individual action items housed within the task note. Many of the task management features (task categorization,  assignment, status and due dates, time due) are not fully benefiting those action items maintained within task notes.

**Hypothesis:** Some PACT teams may want to share tasks across departments.

- ` Not enough information` – We did not encounter teams sharing tasks across departments in this study. 

**Hypothesis:** Some teams may want to support a more "pull-based" workflow, in which tasks as pulled from a common pool of unassigned tasks.

- `Definitely True` – While the majority of care teams participating in the study maintain dedicated patient assignments for team members, 4 of the 17 care teams we spoke with share a pool of tasks that clinicians freely pull from. 

#### Task Management

**Hypothesis:** User workflows may require additional ways to categorize and organize tasks, including potentially more detailed statuses or ways to label tasks. 

- `Definitely True` – Given limitations in how tasks can be  categorized, many clinicians enter descriptive  data into the leading lines in the note field to support task findability. Commonly, clinicians wish to sort or filter by provider, condition, procedure, or next action.

#### Workflow Efficiency

**Hypothesis:** Currently all task details are entered in manually. Users may wish for ways to accelerate their task creation flow with bulk task creation, templatization.

- `Definitely True` – Top features requested include, the ability to enter reoccurring tasks, add standalone subtasks, and append custom reminder to tasks. Other clinicians wished for Task manager to be auto-populated with tasks based on clinical tasks relating to new diagnoses, new entries in the VIRP, new lab results, and upcoming patient appointments.

### Task Manager Use Cases
Through research we uncovered 14 distinct use cases, across various care team roles and functions, who track tasks through TM to:

- Support care management (facilitate continuity of patient care between and after visits)

- And, coordinate clinical processes / workflows

> ##### Use Case: Care Management
>
> One nurse describes it as her provider's brain. If somebody gets a a test done and they need a repeat in a year. We have a whole list of testing like that whether it's colonoscopy, or mammograms. We'll let the patient know that they're due for interval testing and put in an order for it.
>
> For care management or disease management we do a lot of education on lifestyle modification or taking medication. A lot of nurses will use task tracker to follow-up with the patient to see if they've been discharged. We'll also use it to coordinate if the patient needs to see a specialist. A lot of times our patients will get care in the VA, but also outside the VA as well. So we'll use it to track getting records as well. Say you're getting a colonoscopy in two months. I'll put it on my task tracker to request those records so that our system is up to date as well. 
>
> *— P3 Primary Care RN*



> ##### Use Case: Consult/Pre-Op Prep
>
> Even though there's a fair amount of prep that happens before cardiology appointments we don't like to add the orders until we're within a week, because if the vets go and get that done too early, then those results mean nothing to us when we see. 
>
> *— P5 Heart Failure RN*



### Task Manager Use and Team Collaboration

For Task Manager users, the application has quickly become an essential part of the clinical workflow.

> ##### The importance of Task Manager
>
> If the task manager's down. I basically can't do anything. I'll look for voicemails. I'll pull up Word document and put information the same way I have it in my task note and copy it back into CDS later.
>
> — P7 Mental Health RN

Across use cases, clinicians are typically managing assignments dedicated assignments set of specific patients.

Amongst research participants:

- 13 of 17 care teams maintained dedicated patient assignments 
- Care Teams maintaining tasks with shared coverage across team members include general Surgery, Urology, Heart Failure, and CLC Pharmacy
- Tasks are infrequently reassigned outside of coverage planning

> ##### **Dedicated Assignment** 
>
> I've got this weird little coding system that probably doesn't make sense to anybody. When somebody covers me, I will I ask them not to do anything with my CDS tasks. I copy and paste instructions into a Word doc and send very simplified notes. 
>
> — P7 Mental Health RN

> ##### Shared Coverage
>
> There's ACNs and another RN on my team that I will delegate to. I add tasks to our task manager. We'll update the task for each other as we all pick and choose off of it. We don't always stay in our own lane. I might start trying to get ahold of somebody and somebody else will try again. Then a third person will actually finish the follow up. We all use it together.
>
> — P5 Heart Failure RN

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Product Implication:** Task manager is used both for personal task tracking and management as well as team-based task coordination.



### Task Use (Clinician Task Mental Model)

Clinicians in the study prevalently maintain one task per patient. With action items for each patient are typically captured within the task note. As clinicians update the task record, task fields are continuously updated (status, priority, and type).

In addition to tasks clinicians frequently are also shortlisting key patient concerns for patient care, and disease and condition management.

Amongst research participants:

- 21 of 24 care team members maintain 1 task per patient, continuously updated
- The remaining team members in primary care, suicide prevention, and implant coordination manage multiple standalone tasks for patients.  

> ##### 1 task per patient, continuous updated
>
> I track everything, I overdo the heck out of my notes. They're ridiculously detailed. Most of it is just to help me remember things. So I don't have to think about it too much. I don't complete tasks. I just save them for later. I'll bump them out 10 years, just because I'll get the same Veteran a month later. And I'll already have everything here.
>
> — P7 Mental Health RN

> ###### Multiple standalone patient tasks
>
> Yeah, yeah, I guess I I had a very long winded answer of saying that like I document in CPRS and if I need if I need my task tracker to refer to something in CPRS, then I'll reference the the note to look at. I task where I task and I chart where I chart!Yes, I would agree with that.
>
> — P3 Primary Care RN

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> **Product Implication: **Some clinicians maintain 1 task per patient with action items in the task note, others maintain individual standalone tasks with multiple tasks per patient. Product features will need to accommodate both of these types of task use.
>
> Many of the task management features (task categorization, assignment, status and due dates, time due) are not currently benefiting those action items maintained within task notes.



### Tool Use

Clinicians are typically reviewing patient information side by side with reviewing and updating patient tasks.

Additional tools are used for:

- Patient Correspondence
- Care Team Collaboration
- And, Outreach Coordination

Clinician focus is divided across many tools and screens with notifications and updates displayed across multiple touchpoints. Frequently, items need to be tracked in two places and information needs to be collated across touchpoints.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> **Product Implication:** Integration with clinical touchpoint and tools may reduce cognitive effort spent on retrieving patient information, executing action items, coordinating with care team members and corresponding with the patient, and recording outreach attempts.



### Task Filtering Behavior

#### **Prevalence of Due Today, Assigned to Me**

Majority of clinicians are defaulting to filtering task lists to due today, assigned to me.

Clinicians tend to get overwhelmed seeing long lists of items they need to address.

One team opted to create standalone teams for individual members to reduce the overwhelm.

#### **Other Default Time Filtering**

Other use cases may warrant other defaults. As an example, Geriatric Care typically sets their filters to 14 days, as Hospice patients require an interdisciplinary team care plan review every 15 days.

A care coordinator in Urology proposed having default task filtering with due dates set to:

- Due today
- Due tomorrow
- Due next week



### Task Field Use

Clinicians vary in their use of task fields.

##### Status and Due Date

Task status and due date are most prevalent fields used by clinicians. Binary status was mentioned by 4 clinicians (eg. new, complete)1 clinician mentioned not setting status!3 clinicians wished for a status to represent items that were scheduled and pending (separate from in-progress) 

##### Task Type

4 of 16 clinicians mentioned not using task types or task types not being relevant to them. 10 of 19 sessions discussed limitations with task categorization, favoring the description field in providing more granular ways to provide description for tasks. 

##### Priority

Only 3 clinicians mentioned using the priority box. 4 clinicians have requested more granular statuses (eg. red, yellow, green)

##### Time Due

Typically used in conjunction with and scheduled appointment or consult. Not used by 4 of 16 clinicians, 2 clinicians asked to disable this field

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> **Product Implication:** Not every task fields will be relevant to each team. There maybe a desire to set defaults, or disable certain fields. More granularity is needed for task status and task priority.



### Feature Opportunities

Majority of feature requests revolve around task findability and task categorization, and improvements to make task entry less tedious. Additional opportunities relate with surfacing patient information and integration with clinical applications for automated task population and task updates.

#### Opportunity 1: Improved Task Findability

**Many clinicians enter details such as cancer type or provider name into the first line of the notes field so they can scan their task list without opening every task**.

With only one customizable task-type field, clinicians compensate with free text. But that free text isn’t scannable, searchable, or sortable. Many ways that clinicians need to organize tasks have no home in the current UI (ie. condition, provider, urgency, care phase, next action, and etc.)

> ##### Supporting Quote
>
> “For task type, I have phone contact, surveillance, or surgery. Sometimes I get lazy about them. In CMT, they were easier to read because there was a subcategory — surgery, then breast. I would immediately know it was high priority and time sensitive. I could just eyeball it.”
>
> — P11, Nurse Care Coordinator (General Surgery)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Product Implication:** Clinicians would benefit from features to quickly locate and organize tasks across a wide and varied set of categories. This might look like: 
>
> - Free-text tags / labels
> - Additional customizable task fields
> - Searchable description field



#### **Opportunity 2: Patient Information Lookup**

**All Clinicians review patient information and use task manager to short list key patient concerns as they identify clinical tasks, and track and monitor for updates.**

Clinician focus is divided across many tools and screens with individual pieces of patient information gathered from multiple touchpoints. Frequently, items need to be tracked in two places and information needs to be collated across touchpoints. Locating relevant details is cumbersome and time consuming.

> ##### Supporting Quote
>
> "I get all of these radiology alerts with the previous tool we were piloting, we would have it auto populate based on specific radiology diagnostic codes.
>
> Now, Instead. I have to manually enter their name and last four. Go to my processed and figure out the date. It's just a hassle. And we're dealing with an enormous volume."
>
> — P18 Nurse Care Manager PRCC

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> **Product Implication:** Clinicians would benefit from features that surface relevant patient information to enter and update clinical tasks. This might look like: 
>
> - Reverse patient context (action to navigate to relevant patient view in CCOW enabled applications from patient related tasks)
> - dot.phrase integration – pulling structured patient data from the EHR directly into TM task notes.



#### Opportunity 3: Task Entry Enhancements

**All but 3 clinician research participants are capturing patient related sub-tasks within individual tasks. A quarter of clinician research participants, expressed a desire for the ability to capture repeated or related tasks.**

Without a means add individual sub-tasks:

- Some clinicians track subsequent actions within the task note, increasing cognitive load and leaving room for items to be missed
- Some clinicians set reminders separately in CPRS through "ticklers", leading to confusion with tracking the same item through multiple tools
- Some clinicians enter duplicate tasks for repeated items, adding unneeded time and labor to their task management workflows.

> ##### Supporting Quote
>
> Task creation is repetitive. Action items get lost. The cost of entry is too high relative to the value.
>
> "Right now, for each follow-up we're making 2 actual tasks. One task is for the actual appointment. And then we're making another task so we can remember to call the patient the day before."
>
> — P14, Nurse Care Coordinator

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> **Product Implication:** Clinicians would benefit from features to reduce effort to enter tasks while enabling more granular task tracking.
